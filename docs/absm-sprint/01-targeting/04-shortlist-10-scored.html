Reading the scores

Three patterns are visible:

  1. Family-owned mid-band manufacturers cluster at 80+. The matrix rewards exactly the profile we said we wanted: €300M–€1.5B, family-owned, NIS2-obligated, heavy OT. The top 10 includes 9 family-owned companies (Beckhoff is owner-managed; Reinhausen, Schunk, Wittenstein, Witte, EMAG, Lenze, Pilz, Harro Höfliger, Witzenmann all family-controlled).

  2. Above-band companies score lower even when otherwise strong — Phoenix Contact (€3.4B) drops to 64 mostly on Criterion 3. The matrix is doing its job.

  3. The “obvious” sweet spots have been quiet on security publicly. Most score 3 on Criterion 5 because they haven’t publicly discussed NIS2, Zero Trust, or cybersecurity strategy in a way I could find. Stage 2 intel will deepen this — there may be evidence I missed (CISO LinkedIn posts, job listings, etc.).

Confidence flags on the scoring

Where the data was thinner:
- Schunk Group, Lenze, Pilz, Putzmeister, Bizerba, Hermle — revenue figures were industry estimates rather than freshly verified ✅ sources. Stage 2 intel will pin down exact 2024 figures.
- Criterion 4 (Axians reachability) scores for most accounts are 🧠 inferred — based on geographic proximity to Axians offices and general Mittelstand cluster patterns, not specific evidence of a relationship. Stage 2 relationship maps will refine.
- Criterion 5 (Security posture signals) is the hardest to score remotely. Most scores of 3 mean “no public negative signal; nothing publicly visible either way.” Stage 2 will look for CISO LinkedIn posts, security-related job postings, recent SBOM/NIS2 statements.

What changes between this scoring and the final 3 selection

The score is a starting filter, not a verdict. The final 3 selection in 05-final-selection-rationale.md will additionally consider:
- Strategic narrative fit — does the account let us tell the Guardicore + Axians + Mittelstand story well?
- Geographic and sector diversity — we want 3 accounts that together demonstrate range, not 3 mechanical engineering companies in BW
- Axians warm-path realism — even high-scoring accounts may have no real Axians bridge; we’d rather pick a slightly lower-scoring account where Axians has a credible angle
- Showcase complementarity — the 3 must work alongside Trumpf, not overlap with it (Trumpf is BW machine tools, so the 3 should cover other ground)
