### The cross-portfolio referral problem

For an Axians NEO account manager working with Hörmann's Alexandra Kempe to introduce Alain de Pauw's IT Security organization, the following needs to happen internally:

1. **Trigger:** Account manager recognizes a NIS2/security opportunity at Hörmann
2. **Routing:** Account manager has a known internal contact in Axians IT Security
3. **Joint planning:** Both portfolios align on offer, pricing, and engagement model
4. **Customer intro:** Joint meeting positioned to customer as "Axians broadening engagement"
5. **Compensation:** Internal credit-split mechanism prevents one portfolio "stealing" the relationship

**The friction:** Internal cross-portfolio handoff in large multi-portfolio integrators is consistently the weakest link. Account managers are compensated on their own portfolio's revenue; introducing a sister portfolio that may "take customer time" away from their own work is structurally disincentivized unless management actively rewards it.

### The PIP architecture solves exactly this problem

Recall from Stage 0: the Partner Intelligence Program (PIP) is a two-layer Channel ABM initiative:

- **Layer 1 — Partner Intent Routing:** Akamai shares high-intent DACH accounts with Axians, segmented by topic
- **Layer 2 — Account-Based Enablement:** Pre-built sales kits per account, including the cross-portfolio narrative

For Hörmann specifically, PIP Layer 1 would surface Hörmann as "in-market for OT segmentation" and route to **both Axians NEO** (existing relationship) and **Axians IT Security** (target portfolio). PIP Layer 2 would provide pre-built materials specifically designed for the cross-portfolio handoff: a one-page brief for Kempe/Eichler explaining why their colleague Redinger should meet de Pauw's team.

**This is the PIP use case par excellence:** Akamai's intent + content delivery enables Axians to *systematically* do the cross-portfolio orchestration that today happens only sporadically through individual account-manager initiative.
