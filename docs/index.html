A. Marushevsky
Re: Senior Channel Marketing Manager, DACH · Zero Trust

Revolving doors and firewalls have more in common than you'd think. Both protect what matters. Both need a partner network to sell, install, and service. Relationships close deals, but systems are what make the channel scale.

At Boon Edam (revolving doors, security portals, turnstiles) a partner program across Eastern Europe was built from a blank sheet. No inherited pipeline. No existing partners. Every relationship recruited, every onboarding designed, every co-sell motion structured from scratch. The results after four years: 150% of budget. ROS at 24%. COGS down 10.5 percentage points. Not a market tailwind. A system that compounded.

Before that, four years of consulting across enterprise tech ecosystems. ABM programs for partners of Cisco, NetApp, SAP, Dassault Systèmes. Each time the same problem: how do you get independent businesses to sell someone else's technology as if it were their own? The answer was always structural. Ready-made campaign kits. Enablement that doesn't require a PhD in the vendor's product line. Measurement that makes the channel accountable, not just active.

The bridge from physical security to cybersecurity is structural, not cosmetic. Same channel economics: long cycles, technical pre-sales, margin sensitivity, trust that takes years to build. The product changes. The operating system does not.

What follows is not a pitch. Not a diagnosis. Three demonstrations of how the same system-building approach applies to DACH Zero Trust channel development.

What the first 90 days look like

Days 1–30
Listen & Map

Study internal systems and data. Shadow the best-performing partner manager in another region. Meet the DACH partner roster. Map the current landscape against the entanglement research already prepared. Goal: convert outside-in research into an inside-out operating plan.

Days 31–60
Build & Test

Validate partner research with internal data: MDF utilization, deal registration velocity, pipeline per partner. Publish the DACH Ideal Partner Profile. Begin first conversations with priority partners. Draft the H2 campaign calendar anchored to it-sa Nürnberg.

Days 61–90
Pipeline on the board

First joint pipeline review with at least two partners. Activate co-marketing with one. InfoGuard is the lowest-friction first move. Establish the measurement cadence: monthly partner pipeline reviews, quarterly business reviews, shared dashboard for the DACH field team. The 90-day proof point: partner-sourced opportunities in active pipeline.
