Re: Senior Channel Marketing Manager, DACH · Zero Trust

I work in security too — physical security. For the past several years I have been at Boon Edam, a global manufacturer of revolving doors, security portals, and turnstiles. Global clients, global partners. I built the partner program for Eastern Europe from a blank sheet: recruited the partners, designed the onboarding, ran the co-marketing, owned the P&L. The results — 150% budget delivery, return on sales of 24%, COGS reduction of 10.5 percentage points over three years — came from a systematic approach to partner lifecycle management, not from being lucky with the market.

Earlier in my career, in Russia, I worked on ABM strategy implementation projects for Cisco's partner ecosystem. The theme was passwordless authentication. The method was Cisco's own partner portal playbooks — structured campaign kits, partner enablement frameworks, co-selling motions. That experience shaped how I think about giving partners everything they need to succeed: make it ready-made, make it repeatable, let them focus on the customer conversation.

This is not a random career switch. It is an evolution — from physical security channels to cybersecurity channels, from hardware ecosystems to Zero Trust ecosystems, from regional P&L ownership to DACH channel marketing at scale. The problems are structurally the same: recruit the right partners, enable them to sell independently, co-sell on named accounts, and measure everything against pipeline contribution. The product changes. The system does not.

What follows are three perspectives — not a diagnosis, not a critique. These are demonstrations of how I think, what I build, and what I would bring to the role. Each links to the full artifacts behind it.

Three perspectives

Perspective 1
Method & Vision

I developed a 7-stage partner lifecycle methodology with a maturity model that can assess any partner program against three levels: Basic, Professional, and World-class. Each stage has binary capability checkpoints and measurable KPIs. The model creates diagnostic clarity — you see immediately which stages carry the program and which are underbuilt. It works as both a strategic framework for executives and an operational tool for the channel marketing team running it day to day.

Perspective 2
Partner Mapping & Prioritization

I built a 6-dimension scoring model and applied it to 33 DACH cybersecurity partners — scoring each on entanglement depth, vendor diversity, product-gap fit, NIS2 regulatory exposure, organisational elasticity, and geographic coverage. The research includes a competitive entanglement analysis (who is connected to whom across Akamai, Illumio, Palo Alto, Cisco, and Zscaler), 12 deep partner profiles, a recruitability framework, and the logic behind selecting five priority partners with specific outreach rationale.

What the first 90 days look like

Days 1–30
Learn Akamai from the inside

Study internal best practices, systems, and data. Ideally travel to shadow the best-performing partner manager in another region and observe how they work. Meet the existing DACH partner roster. Understand the internal tooling — Partner Hub, deal registration flow, MDF mechanics, 6sense intent data infrastructure. Map the current DACH partner landscape against the entanglement research already prepared. The goal: convert outside-in research into an inside-out operating plan validated by real data.

Days 31–60
Apply the methodology

Validate the partner research with internal data — MDF utilization rates, deal registration velocity, active pipeline per partner. Publish the DACH Ideal Partner Profile internally. Begin first conversations with priority partners: each outreach has a specific hook prepared, a named executive to contact, and a defined next step (not a signed agreement — a joint POC proposal, a co-marketing brief, or a named-account co-sell conversation). Draft the DACH partner campaign calendar for H2 anchored to it-sa Nürnberg.

Days 61–90
First pipeline on the board

First joint pipeline review with at least two partners. Activate co-marketing with one partner — InfoGuard is the lowest-friction first activation (existing Akamai relationship, 350 security experts, expanding into Germany). Establish the measurement cadence: monthly partner-sourced pipeline reviews, quarterly business review calendar, and a shared dashboard giving the DACH field team visibility into partner activity. The 90-day proof point: partner-sourced opportunities in the active pipeline.

The artifacts behind each perspective were built over the past weeks. They represent a first pass based on publicly available information — the methodology, not the conclusions, is the point. With inside data, these become operational tools.
