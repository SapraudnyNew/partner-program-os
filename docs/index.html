Re: Senior Channel Marketing Manager, DACH · Zero Trust

Revolving doors and firewalls have more in common than you'd think. Both protect what matters. Both require a partner ecosystem to sell, install, and service. Both fail when the channel is built on spreadsheets instead of systems.

At Boon Edam, a partner program for physical security products was built from zero across Eastern Europe. The numbers after four years: 150% budget delivery. Return on sales at 24%. COGS reduced by 10.5 percentage points. No inherited pipeline — every partner recruited, onboarded, and developed against a maturity framework.

Before that: ABM implementation for Cisco partners in Russia, focused on passwordless authentication. Different product, same architecture problem — how do you get independent businesses to sell your technology as if it were their own?

The bridge to cybersecurity is structural, not cosmetic. Physical doors and digital perimeters share the same channel economics: long sales cycles, technical pre-sales requirements, partner margin sensitivity, and trust that takes years to compound.

What follows are three perspectives — not a diagnosis, not a critique. These are demonstrations of how the same system-building approach applies to DACH Zero Trust channel development.

What the first 90 days look like

Days 1–30
Learn Akamai from the inside

Study internal best practices, systems, and data. Shadow the best-performing partner manager in another region. Meet the existing DACH partner roster. Map the current landscape against the entanglement research already prepared. Convert outside-in research into an inside-out operating plan validated by real data.

Days 31–60
Apply the methodology

Validate partner research with internal data — MDF utilization rates, deal registration velocity, active pipeline per partner. Publish the DACH Ideal Partner Profile internally. Begin first conversations with priority partners. Draft the DACH partner campaign calendar for H2 anchored to it-sa Nürnberg.

Days 61–90
First pipeline on the board

First joint pipeline review with at least two partners. Activate co-marketing with one partner — InfoGuard is the lowest-friction first activation. Establish measurement cadence: monthly partner-sourced pipeline reviews, quarterly business reviews, and a shared dashboard. The 90-day proof point: partner-sourced opportunities in the active pipeline.
