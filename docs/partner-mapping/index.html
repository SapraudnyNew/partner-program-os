Perspective 2

Partner Mapping & Prioritization

A 6-dimension scoring model applied to 33 DACH cybersecurity partners. Competitive entanglement analysis, recruitability framework, and the logic behind selecting five priority partners.

In this section
  • Ideal Partner Profile — six weighted scoring dimensions for Zero Trust channel partners
  • DACH landscape — 33 security-focused system integrators and MSSPs mapped across Germany, Austria, Switzerland
  • Pursue Five — deep profiles on five recruitment-priority partners with outreach hooks
  • Entanglement analysis — existing vendor relationships, recruitability signals, and disposition logic (Pursue / Contain / Monitor / Drop)

The approach

Channel recruitment without scoring criteria is networking. With scoring criteria, it becomes a pipeline.

An Ideal Partner Profile with six weighted dimensions was built and applied to 33 DACH cybersecurity partners. Each scored on entanglement depth, product-gap fit, NIS2 regulatory exposure, vendor diversity, organisational elasticity, and geographic coverage. Competitive entanglement was mapped across five vendors: Akamai, Illumio, Palo Alto, Cisco, Zscaler. Every partner received a disposition: Pursue, Contain, Monitor, or Drop.

All data sourced from public records. Annual reports, press releases, vendor partner locators, LinkedIn headcount analysis, published case studies. No proprietary information. No insider data. A first pass to demonstrate the methodology, not to deliver a finished recommendation.

Five partners surfaced as priority targets. Each gets a deep-dive: business model, key personnel, vendor entanglements, estimated switching costs, and a recruitability score. The Pursue Five share a common profile: multi-vendor cybersecurity stack with no microsegmentation play, NIS2-exposed customer base, and a structural opening that creates a specific outreach moment.

The six dimensions

Each partner is scored across six dimensions. The dimensions are weighted by their predictive value for partner success — not all six carry equal importance. The weights were calibrated against channel industry benchmarks and direct experience building a partner scoring model at Boon Edam.

D1 · Weight: High
Entanglement Depth

How deeply is the partner embedded with competing vendors? A partner who is a vendor's EMEA Partner of the Year is structurally locked. A partner with shallow multi-vendor relationships is movable.

D2 · Weight: High
Product-Gap Fit

Does the partner's current portfolio have a gap that maps to the vendor's differentiated capabilities? A partner selling endpoint and firewall but not microsegmentation has a natural entry point.

D3 · Weight: Medium
NIS2 Regulatory Exposure

How many of the partner's customers are affected by NIS2 compliance? Partners serving German and Austrian manufacturing, energy, and healthcare have the largest addressable market right now.

D4 · Weight: Medium
Vendor Diversity

Does the partner run a multi-vendor model or is it single-vendor aligned? Multi-vendor partners are better recruits — they have existing processes for adding new technologies.

D5 · Weight: Medium
Organisational Elasticity

Is the partner going through an acquisition, reorganisation, or expansion that creates an opening for new vendor partnerships? Structural change increases receptiveness.

D6 · Weight: Low
Geographic Coverage

Does the partner's footprint cover the target territories — Germany, Austria, Switzerland? DACH-wide coverage scores highest.

The disposition logic

Every partner receives one of four dispositions. The disposition determines investment level — it is an allocation framework, not a value judgment.

Disposition Definition Action
Pursue High fit, low entanglement, open window. Named-executive outreach, tailored pitch, 90-day activation target.
Contain Existing relationship or mixed signals. Deepen rather than recruit. Upgrade tier, activate underused product lines, structured QBR cadence.
Monitor Interesting but timing is wrong, or fit is partial. Passive watch. No dedicated outreach. Revisit in 6 months.
Drop Structurally locked to a competing vendor. No investment. Acknowledge and move on.

Key findings

Of the 33 partners assessed, five received Pursue dispositions, three received Contain, and two were classified as Drop — structurally locked to a competing vendor at the deepest partnership levels. The Pursue Five share a common profile: multi-vendor cybersecurity stack with no microsegmentation play, NIS2-exposed customer base, and an identifiable structural opening (acquisition, expansion, or vendor gap) that creates a specific outreach moment.

Each artifact carries a "first pass from public sources" caveat — these are research demonstrations, not finished intelligence. With internal data (deal registration history, MDF utilisation, partner revenue splits), the scoring model becomes an operational tool.

All partner research is a first pass based on publicly available information, prepared for demonstration of methodology. Scores, dispositions, and outreach recommendations should be validated with internal operational data before any external engagement.
