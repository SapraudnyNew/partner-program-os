1. Purpose and reading guide

This matrix scores 32 DACH cybersecurity / IT-systems-house partners for the strength of their entanglement with Akamai’s competitors across four signal layers (per ADR-011):

  • L1 — Contractual / Distribution: named exclusive or top-tier distribution rights, MSSP-tier program lock.
  • L2 — Equity / Board / Ownership: shareholding by a competitor, board interlock, M&A.
  • L3 — Flagship marketing recognition: Partner-of-the-Year awards, named flagship tiers, co-branded keynote slots.
  • L4 — Executive migration: named senior hires from a competitor in the last 24 months, or named ex-employees moving to a competitor.

Each row also carries an Akamai presence flag (named Akamai tier or public Akamai/Guardicore content on partner site), a Recruitability score (1 low — 5 high; see File 3 framework), and a Disposition (Pursue / Contain / Monitor / Drop; see File 4).

The 4-layer framework deliberately privileges durable signals (contract, equity, flagship status) over reversible ones (marketing slide, single deal). Two-layer or higher competitor entanglement is treated as a structural lock; one-layer entanglement leaves room for re-balancing.

A blind spot worth flagging up-front: the Illumio Enlighten Partner Program publishes only top-tier badges (Spark / Radiate) and partner-of-the-year winners. Mid-tier Illumio partners that are not currently being promoted by Illumio marketing are under-represented in this matrix and will require partner-finder scraping in a follow-up pass.

4. Concentration patterns (read in conjunction with File 2)

Four structural patterns dominate the DACH market and shape every disposition below:

Pattern A — Cisco + PANW + Zscaler triple-anchor. This is the default systemhaus stack: Computacenter, Bechtle, Cancom, Controlware, NTT Data DE, K-Businesscom. Where all three vendors are concurrently named at flagship tier, the partner cannot be “displaced”; Akamai must work within the residual product-gap budget (typically: micro-segmentation for east-west, API security, and DNS/DDoS edge). Recruitability scores cluster at 1-2.

Pattern B — PANW + Fortinet boutique MSSP. Indevis is the archetype: a Munich-based ISO 27001 MSSP with two firewall flagships and no Akamai/Illumio. These partners are structurally locked to firewall-vendor enablement and a 24×7 NOC P&L; they will not switch flagships absent a vendor partnership-program failure (price increases, channel-program contraction). Score 2.

Pattern C — Multi-flagship Mittelstand systemhaus. SVA, Axians/Fernao, ACP, Nomios DE, suresecure, Kudelski. These are the Pursue targets. They carry 3-6 named vendors, no single one at “exclusive” or “Diamond Innovator” tier, and they have publicly recognised cyber depth (ISG Leader, BSI APT-Response, Cyber Trust Label) without being locked into either Illumio or Akamai-segmentation competitors.

Pattern D — Illumio anchor (rare). KAEMI and Navixia (and to a lesser extent AVANTEC) are the only partners where Illumio sits at the top of the vendor totem pole. KAEMI compounds with Cloudflare ASDP. Disposition is Drop or Monitor; tripwires (executive turnover, contract renewal lapse) would re-open dialogue.

  • High confidence (✅) rows: 1-18, 30-33. Primary-source citations available for every L1/L3 claim.
  • Mixed confidence (⚠️/🧠) rows: 19-22, 24-29. Public signal thin or based on group-level inference; pre-engagement diligence will sharpen scores.

ADR-011 should be amended.
