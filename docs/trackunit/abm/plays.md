# Play library

Partner ABM at Trackunit runs in three layers. Each layer is a reusable play with its own objective, audience inside the partner organization, channels, content, metrics and exit criteria. Every metric ties back to the root metric of the role: IrisX credit consumption. Part 1 defines the layers. Part 2 applies them as compact play cards to the five non-showcase accounts from [the First Five](../partner-mapping/first-five.md), sequenced against the [execution plan](../execution/first-five-plan.md).

## Part 1: The three layers

### ABM-to-partner

Marketing to the partner organization itself. The partner is the account: the goal is to get the integration decision made inside their walls before any joint activity exists.

- **Objective.** Move a named partner from first contact to a scoped, resourced integration commitment against the IrisX API surface.
- **Target roles inside the partner org.** Business development or ecosystem owners (the door), product management (the roadmap decision), engineering or technology leadership (the build), and one executive sponsor held in reserve for the escalation moment. Where no partnerships function exists, which the org maps show is the norm, product and BD absorb the role.
- **Channels.** LinkedIn (person-level outreach and commenting on the partner's own campaign content), shared event circuits (bauma, ARA, IRE, CONEXPO and partner user conferences), and exec-to-exec only after working-level traction.
- **Content types.** One-page reference architectures mapped to interfaces the partner already publishes, connector one-pagers per IrisX API surface, delivery-economics briefs (one IrisX feed replacing N per-OEM integrations), and working demo assets for the first meeting.
- **Metrics (assumptions, tied to credit consumption).** Number of accounts moved from first meeting to scoped connector per quarter (each scoped connector carries a projected credit run-rate, labeled assumption); projected credits per scoped integration based on the account's fleet and tenant shape (assumption); days from first meeting to signed scope, since every idle month is unconsumed credits (assumption).
- **Exit criteria.** Done when the integration is scoped and resourced on both sides: hand off to ABM-with-partner. Escalated when working-level contacts engage but stall on commitment: exec-to-exec meeting with the named sponsor. Dropped when two full outreach cycles across at least three named people produce no product-side engagement.

### ABM-with-partner

Joint marketing with the partner to shared and target customers. The integration exists or is in build; the goal is activation.

- **Objective.** Convert a built integration into activated tenants through one repeatable joint go-to-market motion, and produce the first joint reference.
- **Target roles inside the partner org.** Partner marketing and event owners, product marketing, field sales leadership on both sides, and the customer success teams that own the shared install base.
- **Channels.** Joint webinars into the partner's customer base, co-located event presence and joint conference sessions, coordinated marketplace listing launches (Trackunit Marketplace plus the partner's own store), and joint content on LinkedIn timed to launch moments.
- **Content types.** Joint launch announcements, migration or activation playbooks, a first shared customer reference case, webinar decks built on live data, and campaign kits into mutual customer lists.
- **Metrics (assumptions, tied to credit consumption).** Credits from co-marketed tenants within 90 days of the joint launch (target, assumption); activation rate: share of the campaigned install base that turns on the connector, each activation carrying a per-tenant credit baseline (assumption); webinar or event attendees converted to syncing tenants (assumption).
- **Exit criteria.** Done when the joint motion is documented, first credits flow from co-marketed accounts, and one reference case is public: shift to ABM-through-partner. Escalated when the integration is live but joint activation stalls for a quarter: back to the executive sponsor with the consumption gap quantified. A with-partner play never runs on an unbuilt integration; if scope slips, it drops back to ABM-to-partner.

### ABM-through-partner

The partner markets and sells IrisX-backed capability to its own base. The partner becomes the channel.

- **Objective.** Make the partner's own sales and marketing engine the distribution channel, so credit consumption grows without pod involvement per deal.
- **Target roles inside the partner org.** General manager or CRO (channel economics and bundle pricing), sales enablement leads, channel and field marketing, and the partner's own account managers who carry the bundle.
- **Channels.** The partner's sales motion itself, marketplace self-service listings with instrumented activation, through-channel campaign kits the partner runs under its own brand, and the partner's user conference as the annual anchor.
- **Content types.** Enablement kits and battlecards for partner sellers, credit-resale bundle collateral where the model applies, self-service onboarding flows for the connector, and a shared consumption dashboard both sides review quarterly.
- **Metrics (assumptions, tied to credit consumption).** Active tenants sourced by the partner channel and their monthly credit run-rate (assumption); credits per active tenant per month against the reference consumption case for that connector shape (assumption); partner-attached pipeline converted to first credit inside 30 days of go-live, matching the pod KPI (target, assumption).
- **Exit criteria.** Done when the channel reaches steady state: consumption grows quarter over quarter with no pod hours per activation, and the account moves to run-rate management with a quarterly review. Escalated when the partner sells but tenants do not consume: joint activation taskforce with the exec sponsor. Dropped back to ABM-with-partner if the partner's sellers do not carry the bundle after two enablement cycles.
