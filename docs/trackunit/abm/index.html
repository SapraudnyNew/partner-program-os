Partner ABM

Account-based marketing is normally aimed at customer accounts: pick the few that matter, map the buying committee, watch for signals, engage many people at once. Partners are accounts too. A partner decision at a company like Procore or Microsoft involves a committee of product, partnerships, engineering, and commercial owners. The cycle runs quarters, not weeks. And the partnership you want competes against every other priority on their roadmap. So the same discipline applies: deliberate account selection, committee mapping, signal-driven timing, and multithreaded engagement. One thing changes. The win is not a signed deal. The win is a live integration that consumes IrisX credits, because a partnership that produces no consumption is a logo, not a result.

This section takes the five partners selected in the mapping work and runs them through an ABM operating model built for partner accounts. It works in three layers.

The three layers

ABM-to-partner: win the partner organization itself. The partner is the target account. The work is the classic ABM motion pointed at a partner org: map the people who can say yes and the people who can say no, track the triggers that open a window (a product launch, a leadership change, a competitor announcement, a sunset deadline like SAP ETM’s), and run outreach on multiple threads at once so the case does not live or die with one champion. Good looks like this: a named committee for each account with a relationship stage per person, a trigger list that is checked weekly, and at least three active threads into every priority account before the first formal conversation.

ABM-with-partner: sell together into shared customers. Once a partner is signed and the integration is live, the account list changes. Now the targets are joint customer accounts: contractors and equipment owners who run both products but have not activated the connector. The work is joint target selection from customer overlap, shared plays into those accounts, and co-owned follow-through. Good looks like a shared account list both sides have agreed to, a joint play per account segment, and activation tracked per account, because every activated account is a new consumption stream.

ABM-through-partner: the partner as channel. At maturity the partner stops being the target and becomes the route. Marketplace listings, co-marketing into the partner’s install base, and partner-sourced pipeline turn one relationship into distribution. Good looks like a listed connector the partner’s own sellers position, co-marketing motions with measurable response, and a growing share of new consuming tenants that arrive without direct Trackunit effort per deal.

How this maps to the method

The three layers are not a new framework competing with the seven-stage lifecycle. They are the account-level engagement model for it. ABM-to-partner is how Source and Qualify get executed against a specific account: the committee map and trigger list are what turn a scored candidate into a signed scope. ABM-with-partner is the engine behind Launch and Adopt: joint accounts, first credits, then habit. ABM-through-partner is Scale expressed as a channel motion: the partner sells, the connector deploys without custom work, and consumption compounds. Same lifecycle, viewed from inside the account.
