A. Marushevsky
Re: Head of Partnerships, Integrations & Applications · IrisX

I have spent twenty years around construction sites: Hilti systems in the structure, Boon Edam entrances at the front door. The machines on those sites now produce billions of data points a day. Collecting them is solved. The open question is who builds the ecosystem that turns them into recurring platform revenue. That is a partnerships job, and it is a system-building job.

At Boon Edam (security entrances: revolving doors, portals, access) I built the partner program from scratch. Qualification criteria, commercial terms, joint business plans, enablement content partners actually use, certification made mandatory, every partner-sourced deal attributed in CRM. The result: partner-sourced growth of 55% year over year, 150% of budget, with newly onboarded tier-1 partners generating 60% of total marginal growth. The network existed before me. The system that made it produce revenue did not.

Before that, consulting inside enterprise software ecosystems: 30+ account-based programs designed and delivered for partners of Cisco, SAP, and Dassault Systèmes. Every program answered the same question this role asks: how do you get an independent company to build its business on someone else's platform? The answer was never the contract. It was structural: ready-made campaign kits, enablement that respects the partner's time, and measurement that ties partner activity to pipeline, not to activity reports.

And before that, a P&L: managing director at Boon Edam with 24% return on sales, the highest among 19 subsidiaries, and regional leadership at Hilti with 25% ROS and 42% ROCE in the construction industry itself. This matters for one specific reason: IrisX credit consumption is a P&L line, not a partnership vanity metric. Integrations either produce consumption, margin, and defensibility, or they are engineering cost. I read them the way a P&L owner reads them.

One more thing. This site, the research behind it, and the artifacts in every section were assembled by a runbook-driven system of AI agents that I designed and operated, in days. Trackunit shipped an MCP server that puts IrisX data in front of AI agents. I work the same way: the partner's job is the one thing that cannot be automated, showing up and closing. Everything around it can now be built at machine speed.

What follows is not a pitch and not a diagnosis. Three demonstrations of how I would run Integrations & Applications as an engine for IrisX consumption.

What the first 90 days look like

Days 1-30
Listen & Map

Inventory the live integrations and the partner demand backlog. Sit in the P&E and GTM meetings before proposing anything. Meet the partners behind the top connectors and the customers behind the top consumption. Map this outside-in research against inside data. Goal: an integration roadmap draft grounded in demand evidence, not in who asked loudest.

Days 31-60
Stand Up the Machine

Publish the qualification framework and integration roadmap v1, agreed with the Platform Partnerships Lead. Start the weekly pod cadence with structured interlocks into P&E and GTM. Open the first two scoping engagements from the priority list. Define the consumption reporting everyone will be measured on.

Days 61-90
Prove the Loop

First integration moving through build with a certification gate and a launch plan. Marketplace listing flow tested end to end from the partner's side. Credit consumption reporting live at the connector level. The 90-day proof point: partner demand converting into scoped, building, consuming integrations on one board everyone can read.
