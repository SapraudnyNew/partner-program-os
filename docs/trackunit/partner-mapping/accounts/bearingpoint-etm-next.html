Account Plan: BearingPoint (ETM.next)

Account snapshot

BearingPoint is an independent, partner-owned management and technology consultancy headquartered in Amsterdam, with FY2025 revenue of EUR 1.026 billion, bookings near EUR 1.3 billion, 2,200+ projects across 26 countries and roughly 6,200 employees. Beyond consulting it runs a productized software business: ETM.next, a SaaS for equipment and tools management (fleet, rental, tools, logistics, billing) aimed at construction and other asset-heavy industries, sold via SAP Store and operated by BearingPoint Business Services B.V. ETM.next is SAP-endorsed (SAP Pinnacle Award 2021, SAP Endorsed App since April 2022, SAP Spotlight+ designation) and is SAP’s designated industry cloud successor to the legacy SAP ETM module. The product runs on SAP BTP as a side-by-side extension to ERP/S/4HANA and, per its own FAQ, connects to OEM telematics platforms through RESTful OData APIs following AEMP 2.0 and ISO 15143-3. That is exactly the interface IrisX exposes through its ISO 15143-3 (AEMP 2.0) export API, and public sources show no existing ETM.next-Trackunit integration as of July 2026. Every ETM.next migration that lands needs a mixed-fleet telematics feed, which makes this account a direct IrisX credit consumption channel (inference).

Why now

  • 2025: usage rights for legacy SAP Equipment and Tools Management ended in S/4HANA compatibility mode; maintenance in the ERP context ends 2027. Every remaining SAP ETM contractor must re-platform, and 2026-2027 is the peak migration window (inference).
  • 7-13 April 2025: BearingPoint exhibited ETM.next at bauma in Munich (Hall A2 Booth N), after earlier presence at bauma China. Trackunit exhibits on the same event circuit (inference: shared floor, shared audience).
  • 2025-2026: active demand generation against the 2027 deadline, including the four-part blog series “From isolated tools to ETM.next” and an insight arguing that rebuilding custom ECC/ETM code in S/4HANA is a costly mistake. Recent customer win: SACDE, a leading Argentine contractor, went live ahead of its S/4HANA go-live.
  • 5 March 2026: FY2025 results announced, third consecutive year above EUR 1 billion, 1,000+ new hires including 16 new Partners, and a new North America JV with ABeam (“BearingPoint North America”), matched by Chicago-based ETM.next implementation hiring.
  • Ongoing: the migration market is contested. Wynne Systems (RentalResult) and others run explicit “SAP ETM sunset” campaigns against the same install base, so the window to become the default telematics layer in ETM.next blueprints is finite.

Integration angle and entry path

The play: the SAP ETM sunset forces every remaining ETM contractor onto a successor by 2027, ETM.next is the SAP-endorsed successor, and ETM.next already speaks AEMP 2.0 and ISO 15143-3 per its own FAQ. Instead of ETM.next maintaining per-OEM connections, IrisX becomes the single governed telematics feed in every migration: 100+ OEM integrations normalized behind one interface, with credit consumption scaling per connected asset from day one of each go-live. The mapping to IrisX surfaces: the ISO 15143-3 (AEMP 2.0) export and GraphQL API feed fleet position, hours, fuel and status into ETM.next utilization, idle-time and billing logic; the Rental ERP API handles two-way contract and asset sync for the internal rental workflows ETM.next manages; Time Series backs meter-based cost allocation; MCP is a later conversation once a base integration exists (inference on sequencing). The entry sequence by role: first the BD owner on the product side (confirm partnership appetite and the SACDE-style reference motion), then product management (technical fit, reference architecture on SAP BTP via the OData interfaces ETM.next already uses), then the executive Products sponsor for a formal partnership, and in parallel the SAP transformation partners in DACH so the reference architecture lands inside migration blueprints, not after them. One gap to state plainly: the longlist contains no dedicated alliances or partnerships title for ETM.next, and the named ETM.next contact from the web digest, Christoph Kühne (Business Development Manager, named in the SACDE story), did not appear in the scan, so the first calls must establish who actually owns third-party integrations.

First 30 days and main risk

  • Week 1: build a one-page reference architecture, IrisX as the telematics layer for ETM.next, mapped to their published OData/AEMP 2.0 interface; validate internally which Trackunit customers run SAP ETM or ETM.next today.
  • Week 1-2: ask Trackunit sales and customer success for warm paths into BearingPoint, and check for shared contractor accounts (Eiffage, Graham Construction, SACDE-type profiles) that could anchor a joint reference.
  • Week 2-3: first outreach to Ernest de Weert (BD) and, via the SACDE story contact route, confirm who owns ETM.next third-party integrations; parallel note to Helmut Ritter’s SAP transformation practice.
  • Week 3-4: technical validation call with Frank Zeidler’s product management team; scope a proof of concept feeding one mixed fleet into an ETM.next sandbox via the ISO 15143-3 export.
  • Week 4: decide the escalation to Donald Wachs based on BD and product response; book a bauma-circuit or joint-event follow-up as the co-marketing hook.
  • Main risk: BearingPoint treats telematics as a commodity input, keeps per-OEM OData connections, and sees no reason to standardize on one aggregator, especially while Wynne and STAEDEAN court the same install base. Mitigation: sell delivery economics to the practice, not just the product team; one IrisX connection replacing N per-OEM integrations shortens their standard 2-3 month fast-track implementation and gives their consultants a repeatable blueprint, which is worth more to a partner-owned consultancy than the data feed itself (inference).
