# Akamai HVO — Context

> **Role:** Senior Channel Marketing Manager
> **Location:** DACH region
> **Product focus:** Zero Trust security solutions
> **Posting:** https://jobs.akamai.com/en/sites/CX_1/job/2855

## Entry path

**Warm referral.** A trusted Akamai employee will recommend Alex internally. That contact has already indicated Akamai "will at least take a call." This entry path determines the shape of the HVO (see ADR-006).

## Reader stack

1. **The referrer** — reads the HVO before passing it on. Must feel comfortable championing it. Their credibility is on the line.
2. **The hiring manager** — Senior Manager level, owner of the DACH channel marketing P&L for Zero Trust. Reads to decide whether to schedule the call and what to talk about.
3. **The skip-level (VP Channel Marketing EMEA, or equivalent)** — does not receive the document directly. May read it if the hiring manager forwards it. Document must withstand executive scrutiny.

## Why this role makes strategic sense for Alex

The role is below Alex's career altitude on paper. But three factors make it the right door:

1. **Domain entry, not domain demotion.** DACH cybersecurity channel marketing is a domain Alex has not formally held. Senior Manager is the right rank for a domain-entry move. Trying to enter at Director level would require Akamai to take a bet they have no reason to take.
2. **The role is closer to P&L than the title suggests.** Channel marketing managers at Akamai own MDF allocation, partner co-marketing budgets, pipeline contribution targets. This is small-P&L work — exactly the territory where Alex's 20+ years of P&L thinking compounds quickly.
3. **DACH market access.** A senior role at Akamai in DACH puts Alex inside the European cybersecurity ecosystem with one of the strongest channel programs in the industry. From there, lateral and upward moves become available that don't exist from outside.

## Strategic frame for the HVO

The HVO's central claim: **"Most channel marketers know channel marketing. Few think in P&L. I do both."**

This frame:
- Positions Alex at the right level for the job (channel marketing competence is the table stakes).
- Distinguishes from the typical Senior Manager candidate (they don't have P&L muscle).
- Reads upward to the VP (they think in P&L; they recognize the language).
- Doesn't threaten the hiring manager (P&L thinking is presented as a service to the channel function, not as a power move).
