# Stage 3: Enable

## Strategic intent

Build the partner's capacity to sell and deliver independently. Without manufacturer hand-holding.

Enable is the stage that determines whether a partner program scales or stays founder-dependent. A company can recruit the right partners and onboard them efficiently, but if those partners cannot qualify opportunities, configure solutions, handle objections, position against competitors, and close deals on their own, every sale requires manufacturer involvement. That involvement is expensive, slow, and impossible to scale past a handful of partners.

The economics are stark. Research across thousands of partner programs shows that partners who complete certification programs generate 6x more revenue than uncertified partners. Certified partners register 4x more deals per quarter, run 9x more marketing campaigns, and achieve 2.8x higher win rates on partner-attributed deals. Teams that actively share up-to-date enablement materials hit 82% of quota versus 32% for teams that do not. These are not marginal differences. They are the difference between a partner program that compounds revenue and one that consumes cost. [P-40]

Enablement is not a training department. It is the operating system that transfers capability from the manufacturer to the partner at scale. It covers four domains: product knowledge, sales methodology, marketing execution, and technical competence. The channel marketing function sits at the center of this stage. ## The system

### The four marketing motions

Channel marketing operates through four distinct motions. Each motion serves a different purpose, targets a different audience, and requires different content and measurement. Most programs run one or two motions (usually TO and a fragmented version of WITH). Programs that run all four create a marketing engine that multiplies through the partner base rather than just adding to it. [P-31] [P-32] [P-33] [P-34] [P-35]

**Motion 1: marketing TO partners**

Audience: the partner organization (sales reps, technical staff, management).
Purpose: keep the partner informed, trained, and motivated to sell the manufacturer's products.
Content: product updates, competitive intelligence, training modules, certification programs, partner newsletters, recognition programs, internal incentive announcements.
Measurement: newsletter open rates, training completion, certification rates, portal engagement, event attendance.
Cadence: continuous. Monthly newsletter minimum. Quarterly product update. Annual partner event or advisory board.

This is the foundation. Without marketing TO partners, the partner's sales team forgets the manufacturer exists between deals. The battle for partner mindshare is fought here. Every competitor sends newsletters and training invitations. The differentiator is relevance: content that helps the partner sell more, not content that broadcasts the manufacturer's press releases.

**Motion 2: marketing WITH partners**

Audience: the partner's customers and the manufacturer's customers (joint target audience).
Purpose: generate pipeline through co-branded campaigns, co-funded events, and joint content.
Content: co-branded webinars, joint case studies, co-authored white papers, shared event sponsorships, co-funded demand generation campaigns.
Measurement: pipeline generated from co-marketing campaigns, MDF ROI (revenue attributed to MDF-funded campaigns divided by MDF spent), lead quality and conversion rate.
Investment: Market Development Funds (MDF) allocated per partner with pre-agreed ROI requirements and post-campaign measurement. Every MDF dollar has a pipeline target attached. No MDF without measurement. [P-35]

This is where the partnership becomes visible to the market. Co-marketing signals to end-customers that the partner and manufacturer are aligned. It also tests the partner's marketing execution capability: a partner who cannot execute a co-branded webinar will not execute an independent campaign.

**Motion 3: marketing THROUGH partners**

Audience: the partner's customers (partner's brand, manufacturer's content).
Purpose: scale the manufacturer's message through the partner's distribution channel and customer relationships.
Content: white-label content (the manufacturer writes it, the partner brands it and distributes it), campaign-in-a-box kits, email templates the partner sends from their own platform, social media content the partner publishes under their brand.
Measurement: partner content usage rate (how many partners actually use the provided content), reach (audience size reached through partner distribution), pipeline influenced through partner-distributed content.

This motion scales marketing beyond the manufacturer's own channels. The manufacturer creates the content once. Partners distribute it to their combined audience. The math: if the manufacturer has 50 partners, each with a customer base of 500, marketing THROUGH partners reaches 25,000 contacts through trusted relationships. The manufacturer's own email list cannot replicate that trust signal.

**Motion 4: marketing FOR partners**

Audience: the partner's customers (manufacturer runs the campaign on behalf of the partner).
Purpose: provide marketing execution for smaller partners who lack in-house marketing capability.
Content: the manufacturer runs demand generation campaigns on behalf of the partner, using the partner's brand and targeting the partner's territory. Leads are delivered to the partner's sales team.
Measurement: leads generated for the partner, partner activation (does the partner follow up on the leads), pipeline created from manufacturer-generated leads.

This motion exists because most partners are small businesses. They have sales capability but not marketing capability. Telling them to "run a campaign" produces nothing. Running the campaign for them and handing them qualified leads produces pipeline. The manufacturer absorbs the marketing cost. The partner converts the leads. Both earn revenue. This is asymmetric giving applied to marketing. [P-48]

**The antipattern: random acts of marketing.** Every marketing dollar spent on partner campaigns must tie to a measurable outcome. Co-branded events without pipeline targets, MDF disbursed without post-campaign measurement, and content created without usage tracking are random acts of marketing. They feel productive. They produce nothing measurable. The cure is simple: no campaign launches without a pipeline target, no MDF disburses without a measurement plan, no content publishes without a usage dashboard. [P-35]

### Content architecture

Partner-facing content is not a dump of everything the manufacturer produces. It is a curated library organized by three axes:

**Axis 1: sales cycle stage.**

| Stage | Content type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | ICP definitions, territory data, account mapping output | Help the partner identify who to call |
| Discovery | Discovery question guides, industry pain point briefs | Help the partner qualify the opportunity |
| Solution design | Technical specs, configuration guides, reference architectures | Help the partner scope the solution |
| Proposal | Proposal templates, ROI calculators, pricing guides | Help the partner build the business case |
| Competitive | Battle cards, competitive comparison matrices, win/loss analysis | Help the partner position against alternatives |
| Closing | Customer testimonials, case studies, reference customers | Help the partner overcome final objections |
| Post-sale | Installation guides, handover checklists, service documentation | Help the partner deliver (bridges to Stage 5) |

**Axis 2: partner type.** Not every partner needs every content item. An SI needs reference architectures and integration guides. A reseller needs battle cards and pricing guides. An LSP needs service protocols and parts catalogs. Tag every content item by partner type applicability. [P-13]

**Axis 3: freshness.** Content older than 6 months gets reviewed. Content older than 12 months gets archived or refreshed. Competitive battle cards are refreshed within 30 days of any competitor product launch, pricing change, or major win/loss shift. A partner who downloads a battle card referencing a competitor's product version from two years ago will not trust the next piece of content the manufacturer provides.

**Content findability.** The content library fails if partners cannot find what they need within 60 seconds. Search, tagging, and navigation must be designed for a partner who visits the portal once a month, not for a channel manager who lives in it daily. Research shows that deal velocity improves when partners can access sales-ready content at the point of customer engagement rather than recalling it from a training session completed weeks earlier. Self-service at the moment of need beats training for retention.

### Certification program design

Certification serves three purposes: it validates partner competency, it creates incentive for training completion, and it gives partners a credential they can market to their own customers. A Gold Certified Partner badge on a partner's website is worth more to that partner than any internal report card.

**Certification architecture:**

| Level | Target audience | Requirements | Validity | Benefits unlocked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | All partner sales reps | Core product training + assessment (pass rate: 80%) | 12 months | Deal registration access, standard pricing |
| Advanced | Experienced partner reps | Foundation + advanced modules + practical assessment | 12 months | Priority deal support, enhanced margins, co-marketing eligibility |
| Expert | Senior partner reps and technical leads | Advanced + specialization track + project-based assessment | 24 months | Named account access, joint solution development, speaking opportunities, premium MDF allocation |

**Certification-to-revenue correlation tracking.** This is the measurement that justifies enablement investment. Tag every partner and every opportunity with certification status. Compare certified vs uncertified partners on: deals registered per quarter, win rate, average deal size, sales cycle length, and annual revenue. Report the delta monthly. If certification does not correlate with better performance, the certification program needs redesign, not more investment. If it does correlate (and it almost always does: 6x revenue, 2.8x win rate at best-in-class), the data becomes the justification for expanding the program.

**Recertification.** Certifications expire. Markets change, products evolve, competitors shift. A certification earned 18 months ago on a product version that no longer exists provides false confidence. Annual recertification for Foundation and Advanced. Biennial for Expert. Recertification is lighter than initial certification (update modules only, not full curriculum).

### Internal enablement: training the manufacturer's own team

Enablement is not only partner-facing. The manufacturer's sales team, customer success team, and technical team must also be enabled to work with partners. Common failures:

- Direct sales reps view partners as competition for commission, not as force multipliers. Internal enablement teaches reps how to co-sell with partners, how to use partner data in deal strategy, and how partner-influenced deals convert at higher rates.
- Customer success teams discover partner-installed products only when something breaks. Internal training includes partner identification in account reviews.
- Product teams build without considering partner integration requirements. Partner requirements appear in product roadmap reviews.

Bob Moore's playbook from Procore: share internal win stories about partnership successes in sales training sessions. Include partner managers in forecast and deal calls. When reps report difficulty with an account, partner team members pull up account mapping data to check which partners have relationships with that account. Public accountability drives adoption faster than policy mandates.

## Common failure modes

**Content graveyard.** The portal has 500 documents. Partners download 12 of them. The rest are outdated, unsearchable, or irrelevant to how partners actually sell. The cure is curation (tag, organize, sunset) not creation (add more documents).

**Certification without consequence.** The manufacturer builds a certification program but does not tie it to benefits. Certified and uncertified partners receive the same pricing, same support, same deal access. Certification becomes a checkbox, not a differentiator. The fix: hard-gate benefits behind certification. No co-marketing MDF without Advanced certification. No named account access without Expert. [P-12]

**Random acts of marketing.** MDF spent on partner events and campaigns with no pipeline target, no measurement plan, and no post-campaign review. The manufacturer reports MDF utilization (dollars spent) instead of MDF ROI (revenue generated). Utilization looks productive. ROI exposes waste. [P-35]

**Enablement-as-event.** Annual partner conference plus quarterly webinar equals "enablement program." This covers 4 days out of 365. The other 361 days, the partner sells from memory. Enablement is continuous: monthly content refresh, quarterly battle card update, certification modules available on demand, in-workflow access to competitive positioning during live customer conversations.

**No correlation tracking.** The manufacturer measures training completions (activity metric) but not revenue per certified partner (outcome metric). Without correlation, enablement cannot prove ROI to finance. Without proving ROI, enablement loses budget. Without budget, enablement loses capability. The cycle spirals. Break it by connecting the LMS to the CRM.

## Diagnostic questions

1. What percentage of your partners' sales reps have completed product training? Certification?
2. Do certified partners generate more revenue per rep than uncertified partners? Do you track this?
3. How is your partner-facing content organized? By product? By sales cycle stage? By partner type?
4. When was the last time you retired outdated content from the partner portal?
5. Which of the four marketing motions (TO, WITH, THROUGH, FOR) are you running today? Which are producing measurable pipeline?
6. How do you allocate MDF? What is the ROI requirement? How do you measure post-campaign results?
7. Does your certification program unlock tangible benefits (pricing, support, deal access), or is it informational only?
8. How does your direct sales team view partners? As competitors for commission or as force multipliers?
9. Do you have a self-service enablement platform, or does partner support require manufacturer staff time for every request?
10. How do you gather partner feedback on the enablement experience? How does that feedback influence your content roadmap?

## Intervention library

### Play: four-motion marketing audit

- **Applicability:** any company running partner marketing without a framework. The starting point for Professional maturity.
- **Description:** map every current partner marketing activity to one of the four motions (TO/WITH/THROUGH/FOR). Identify which motions are active, which are absent, and which are running without measurement. Common finding: most companies run Marketing TO (newsletters, training) and fragmented Marketing WITH (occasional co-branded events) but have no Marketing THROUGH or FOR motions. Build a 90-day plan to activate the missing motions, starting with the one that serves the largest partner segment.
- **Source:** [P-31] [P-32] [P-33] [P-34] Marketing Multiplied (Moore/Thomas) four-pillar framework.
- **Lead time:** 2 weeks for audit. 4 weeks for first missing motion activation.

### Play: content architecture rebuild

- **Applicability:** companies with a content library that partners do not use. Symptoms: low portal engagement, high support ticket volume for questions the content should answer, partner feedback that they "can't find anything."
- **Description:** audit every document in the partner portal. Tag by sales cycle stage, partner type, and creation date. Archive anything older than 12 months without refresh. Reorganize by the three-axis structure (stage, type, freshness). Implement search. Add usage tracking (which documents are viewed, downloaded, and used in deal context). Identify the 20 documents that matter most and ensure they are current, findable, and formatted for mobile. Test with 5 partners before full rollout.
- **Source:** [P-40] (institutional capability). Continu research: deal velocity improves when partners access sales-ready content at point of engagement.
- **Lead time:** 4-6 weeks for audit and reorganization. Impact measurable within one quarter via portal engagement metrics.

### Play: certification program launch

- **Applicability:** companies without a formalized certification. Professional maturity play.
- **Description:** build a three-tier certification (Foundation, Advanced, Expert) using the architecture in this document. Tie each tier to specific, tangible benefits. Foundation unlocks deal registration. Advanced unlocks enhanced margins and co-marketing MDF. Expert unlocks named account access and premium support. Build the LMS content (can start with recorded video modules and assessments; live training is a Phase 2 enhancement). Track completion rates and immediately begin tracking the revenue delta between certified and uncertified partners. Report the delta to leadership monthly.
- **Source:** [P-12] (tiers reflect investment). PartnerStack: 6x revenue certified vs uncertified. Konstantly: certified partners register 4.8 deals per quarter vs 1.2.
- **Lead time:** 6-8 weeks for curriculum build and LMS configuration. First certifications within 90 days. Revenue correlation data within 6 months.

### Play: MDF ROI enforcement

- **Applicability:** companies that allocate MDF without measuring return. The play that converts a cost line into a revenue driver.
- **Description:** implement three rules. Rule 1: no MDF disbursement without a pre-approved campaign brief that includes a pipeline target. Rule 2: post-campaign measurement required within 30 days of campaign completion. Rule 3: future MDF allocation weighted by historical ROI. Partners who generate >3x ROI receive increased allocation. Partners who generate <1x receive reduced allocation or mandatory coaching before next disbursement. Publish the rules to all partners. Apply consistently. [P-35]
- **Source:** Marketing Multiplied (Moore/Thomas): "random acts of marketing" is the antipattern. Forrester TEI: Impartner 296% ROI, Partnerize 330% ROI from disciplined partner marketing investment.
- **Lead time:** 2 weeks to define rules and templates. One MDF cycle (typically quarterly) to establish baseline. Visible ROI improvement within two cycles.

### Play: self-service enablement platform

- **Applicability:** companies at Professional maturity with established content and certification, looking to reach World-class. The infrastructure exists but partner support still requires manufacturer staff time for routine requests.
- **Description:** move from portal-as-repository to platform-as-service. Requirements: searchable knowledge base with AI-assisted content recommendations, in-workflow access (partner can access competitive positioning during a live customer call without leaving their sales tool), on-demand training with micro-modules (5-10 minutes, not 60-minute webinars), real-time certification status visible to both partner and manufacturer, automated content delivery triggered by partner activity (new deal registration triggers relevant case study delivery). Integration with CRM and PRM is mandatory: training completion triggers workflow, certification status appears on partner records, content usage correlates with deal outcomes in reporting.
- **Source:** [P-40] (institutional capability). Continu research: modern platforms reduce onboarding time by 40-52%, deliver 296-490% ROI. MatrixFlows: content engagement connected to deal outcomes enables identifying which resources drive pipeline.
- **Lead time:** 8-12 weeks for platform selection and initial configuration. 90 days for partner adoption. Full impact measurable after 6 months of usage data.

### Play: internal co-sell enablement

- **Applicability:** companies where the direct sales team views partners as competition. The play that unlocks co-selling by changing internal behavior.
- **Description:** three actions. First, include partner win stories in internal sales training: specific case studies showing how partner involvement shortened the cycle, increased deal size, or won a competitive displacement. Second, add partner data to deal reviews: when a rep discusses a stalled account, the partner manager checks which partners have relationships with that account and offers introductions. Third, align incentives: direct reps receive commission credit on partner-influenced deals, not just partner-sourced deals. This eliminates the zero-sum perception. Bob Moore (Ecosystem-Led Growth): "public accountability drives adoption faster than policy mandates."
- **Source:** [P-06] (partner data must pierce the veil and reach customer-facing roles). Bob Moore: Procore playbook for internal ELG training.
- **Lead time:** 30 days for first win story presentation. 60 days for deal review integration. 90 days for incentive realignment (requires sales leadership sign-off).

| Internal co-sell training | C | R, A | C | N/A |

**RACI variant: companies without dedicated channel marketing**

In companies where channel marketing does not exist as a separate function, the partner manager absorbs marketing responsibilities. This limits the program to Motion 1 (TO) and fragmented Motion 2 (WITH). Motions 3 (THROUGH) and 4 (FOR) require dedicated marketing execution capacity.

| Activity | First partner hire / CRO | Corporate marketing | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content curation | R (selects from existing corporate content) | A (produces source content) | I |
| Training delivery | R, A (can use recorded modules) | C | R (completes) |
| Co-marketing | R (partner coordination) | R, A (campaign execution) | R (co-executes) |
| MDF allocation | R, A (if budget exists) | C | R (executes) |

The scaling trigger for dedicated channel marketing: when the program exceeds 30 active partners or when partner-sourced revenue exceeds 10% of total revenue, a dedicated channel marketing resource (even 0.5 FTE) produces disproportionate returns. This is the role the Akamai HVO targets: the person who owns the four marketing motions and the content-to-revenue correlation.
