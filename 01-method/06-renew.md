# Stage 6: Renew

## Strategic intent

Monetize the installed base through service revenue. Maintenance contracts, ad-hoc call-outs, spare parts, retrofit, modernization, remote monitoring, and digital services. Contract renewal is one stream among seven.

Renew is not about renewing a contract. It is about building a service business on top of the installed base that Stage 5 created. For industrial B2B manufacturers, service revenue represents 30% or more of total income at top-performing companies. Service gross margins run 2x equipment margins. The EBIT margin from service is 3-7x higher than from new equipment sales. Service growth exceeds new equipment sales growth (10% annually vs 2%). These are not aspirational numbers. They are BCG benchmarks across hundreds of industrial manufacturers.

The installed base is the gold. Every unit delivered and registered in Stage 5 becomes a renewable, serviceable, upgradeable asset. Companies with 80%+ of their installed base under service contracts generate predictable recurring revenue that cushions against economic volatility. Companies with low service penetration leave revenue on the table and expose their installed base to third-party service providers and competitors who will capture the relationship. [P-44]

The partner's role in this stage depends on the service delivery model the company chooses. Some manufacturers deliver service through partners. Some deliver service directly while the partner manages the customer relationship. Some use a tiered model where certified partners deliver autonomously and non-certified partners dispatch to the manufacturer. The service delivery model is defined during program design (intake questionnaire) and determines the RACI, the economics, and the partner capability requirements for this entire stage.

## The system

### Seven service revenue streams

Service revenue is not one line item. It is seven distinct streams, each with different economics, different triggers, different partner roles, and different measurement.

| Stream | Description | Trigger | Revenue model | Typical gross margin | Partner role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Maintenance contracts | Planned, recurring service agreements with defined scope, frequency, and SLAs. At World-class maturity: performance-based contracts tied to uptime/availability outcomes. | Time-based: annual contract, renewed at expiration. Attached at point of sale or sold after installation. | Recurring annual fee. Tiered by SLA level. World-class: fee linked to MTBC/availability metrics. | 30-40% (standard). 40-50% (performance-based, higher value capture). | Delivery model dependent |
| 2. Ad-hoc call-outs | Unplanned break-fix and emergency service | Event-based: equipment failure or urgent need | Per-incident fee: labor + travel + parts. Premium for emergency. | 40-60% | Delivery model dependent |
| 3. Spare parts and consumables | Replacement components and wear parts | Usage-based: parts wear on predictable schedule. Also triggered by service visits. | Parts sale: manufacturer to partner (wholesale), partner to end-customer (retail). | 50%+ | Partner resells (Model 1) or manufacturer sells direct |
| 4. Retrofit | Mid-lifecycle upgrade with newer modules or features | Technology-driven or regulatory-driven | Project-based: hardware + installation labor | 35-45% | Partner identifies during service visit, delivers installation |
| 5. Modernization | Full system refresh at end of lifecycle | Lifecycle-driven: equipment reaches end of usable life (10-20 years) | Capital sale from incumbent position | 25-35% (but higher win rate) | Partner manages relationship, bridges to Stage 4 for the deal |
| 6. Remote monitoring | Connected equipment transmits performance data for anomaly detection | Technology-driven: requires IoT infrastructure on equipment | Subscription fee, often bundled with maintenance as premium tier | 60-80% | Manufacturer owns platform, partner executes field response |
| 7. Digital services | Predictive maintenance, digital twin, AI-driven diagnostics, fleet management dashboards, data-driven advisory | Infrastructure-driven: requires connected equipment (Stream 6) as prerequisite. Layered on top of monitoring data. | Subscription (monthly/annual). Tiered packages: basic monitoring, predictive analytics, full digital twin. Freemium for basic, paid for advanced. | 70-85% (software-like margins once platform exists) | Manufacturer owns platform and data. Partner consumes insights for service delivery. Partner may resell digital subscriptions to end-customers. |

**Stream 1 maturity: from maintenance to performance-based contracts**

Maintenance contracts evolve through three levels that mirror the overall maturity model:

| Level | Contract type | Pricing logic | Risk allocation | Measurement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | Time-and-materials | Fixed fee for scheduled visits. Parts and ad-hoc labor billed separately. | Customer bears all risk of failure. | Visit completion rate, SLA compliance. |
| Professional | Outcome-oriented SLA | Fixed fee covers scheduled maintenance + defined SLA for response time and resolution. Parts may be included or capped. | Shared risk: manufacturer/partner guarantees response time, not uptime. | Response time SLA compliance, first-time fix rate, contract renewal rate. |
| World-class | Performance-based | Fee tied to measurable outcomes: equipment availability (e.g., 99.5% uptime), MTBC targets (e.g., <2 breakdowns per unit per year), or cost-per-use. Fee automatically adjusts if targets are missed. | Manufacturer/partner bears uptime risk. Fee decreases if performance drops. | Availability %, MTBC, MTTR, customer-reported incidents, NPS. |

Performance-based contracts require digital infrastructure (Streams 6 and 7) to function. Without real-time monitoring data, uptime claims cannot be verified. This is why the elevator industry leaders who pioneered performance contracts (KONE, Otis, Schindler) invested heavily in IoT connectivity first. The contract model follows the data model, not the other way around.

**Revenue mix at maturity:** BCG data shows top-performing manufacturers derive service revenue approximately: spare parts 30-35%, maintenance contracts 25-30%, ad-hoc call-outs 10-15%, retrofit and modernization 10-15%, digital services (remote monitoring + predictive + advisory) 10-15% and growing fastest. 55% of North American manufacturers plan to use industrial SaaS models for service growth (BCG/National Association of Manufacturers). The digital services share must reach 10%+ of total company revenue to impact market valuation (BCG).

### Three service delivery models

Define during program design. Applies across all seven streams.

**Model 1: partner delivers service**

Partner technicians perform all on-site work. Manufacturer provides parts, documentation, training, and level-2 escalation. Partner owns the customer service relationship and retains service revenue. Manufacturer earns parts revenue and contract administration fees.

Example companies: Daikin (service partner network with training academy and certification tiers). Boon Edam (partners deliver service and purchase spare parts from manufacturer for resale). Hilti energy and industry division (certified partners deliver with manufacturer quality oversight).

**Model 2: manufacturer delivers service, partner dispatches**

Manufacturer's own service organization performs the work. Partner manages customer communication, dispatches requests, provides triage. Partner earns dispatch fee or commission on contracts sold.

Example companies: KONE, Otis (elevator manufacturers with global direct service teams; partners sell equipment, manufacturer services it). Large automation OEMs with dedicated field service.

**Model 3: tier-dependent hybrid**

Certified partners (LSPs, specialized SIs) deliver autonomously within certification scope. Non-certified partners dispatch to manufacturer or refer to certified partner. Certification level determines which streams the partner can execute.

Example companies: Cisco (Partner Support Services: resell, co-deliver, or partner-delivered options). Dell Technologies (Services Delivery Competencies determine partner autonomy level). Honeywell Aerospace (licensed channel partners by type: dealer, distributor, MRO).

**Intake questionnaire addition:** Who performs field service today? What percentage is partner-delivered vs direct? Do you have service certification tiers? How do you supply spare parts? What is your target model in 12 months?

### Stream 7: digital services in depth

Digital services are the fastest-growing and highest-margin service stream. They require connected equipment (Stream 6 infrastructure) as a prerequisite and layer intelligence on top of monitoring data to create subscription-revenue products.

**Digital service product stack:**

| Tier | Product | What it does | Pricing model | Prerequisites |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Remote monitoring and alerting | Equipment sends health data to cloud. Anomalies trigger alerts to manufacturer and/or partner service team. Customer receives status dashboard. | Included in premium maintenance contract or sold as standalone subscription ($X/unit/month) | IoT sensors on equipment. Cloud platform. Data pipeline. |
| Advanced | Predictive maintenance | AI analyzes usage patterns, vibration, temperature, component cycles to predict failures before they occur. Service is scheduled based on predicted need, not calendar. | Subscription addon. Higher than monitoring because it reduces unplanned downtime. | Foundation tier operational. 6-12 months of historical data for model training. |
| Premium | Digital twin and advisory | Virtual replica of installed equipment mirrors physical state. Enables simulation of changes, optimization of settings, and scenario planning. Advisory services use aggregated fleet data to recommend operational improvements. | Subscription (premium tier) or project-based advisory fees. | Advanced tier operational. Sufficient fleet size for meaningful aggregation (100+ units). |

**The economics of digital services:**

BCG research shows that industrial SaaS business models catalyze faster growth, yield higher margins, and provide less-cyclical revenue. The break-even on a digital services platform takes 3-4 years because infrastructure costs are front-loaded while subscription revenue accrues over the equipment lifecycle. Once the platform exists, marginal cost per additional connected unit is near zero. This creates a J-curve: initial investment followed by accelerating returns as the connected fleet grows.

55% of North American manufacturers plan to use industrial SaaS models for growth. The ones that succeed share three characteristics: their products play a central role in customer operations, their products are connected and upgradeable, and they have direct or partner-mediated relationships with end-customers that create a channel for digital service delivery.

**Partner role in digital services:**

The manufacturer owns the platform, the data, and the AI models. The partner's role shifts:

- In Model 1 (partner delivers): the partner receives predictive alerts and acts on them. The partner's service quality improves (fewer emergency call-outs, higher first-time fix rate) because the system tells them what will fail before it does. The partner may resell digital subscriptions to end-customers as part of a bundled service offering.
- In Model 2 (manufacturer delivers): digital services are a manufacturer-direct offering. The partner's role is limited to customer relationship management and contract sales.
- In Model 3 (hybrid): certified partners access the digital platform. Non-certified partners do not. This creates a powerful incentive for certification: digital access is the premium benefit of the highest tier.

### Digital maturity layer: how digital upgrades every stream

Digital capabilities do not exist in isolation. They enhance every other service stream. This is the overlay that separates Professional from World-class across the entire stage.

| Stream | Without digital (Professional) | With digital (World-class) | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Maintenance contracts | Calendar-based scheduled visits. Same interval regardless of equipment condition. | Condition-based maintenance: service when the data says it is needed, not when the calendar says. Performance-based contracts become possible because uptime is measurable. | 30-50% reduction in unnecessary visits. Higher contract value (outcome-based pricing). KONE: 70% increase in proactive fault identification. |
| 2. Ad-hoc call-outs | Reactive: customer calls, technician dispatched. Diagnosis starts on arrival. | Predictive: system alerts before failure. Remote diagnostics triage the issue before the technician arrives. Technician arrives with the right part and the right knowledge. | TK Elevator MAX: 50% downtime reduction. Otis ONE: technician arrives pre-informed. First-time fix rate increases. |
| 3. Spare parts | Reactive ordering: parts ordered when needed, lead time variable. | Predictive parts demand: system forecasts which parts will be needed based on component cycle counts and wear patterns. Parts pre-positioned at partner or customer site. | Reduced stockouts. Reduced excess inventory. Higher capture rate (parts ordered through manufacturer system, not third-party). |
| 4. Retrofit | Identified during service visits based on technician observation. | Identified by data: system detects declining performance that maintenance cannot address. Retrofit recommendation generated with ROI based on actual operational data. | Earlier identification. Stronger business case (data-backed ROI vs technician opinion). Higher conversion. |
| 5. Modernization | Triggered by age-based rules or customer request. | Triggered by total cost of ownership analysis: system compares cumulative maintenance cost against replacement cost in real time. Alert at the crossover point. | Proactive modernization proposal at the economically optimal moment. Competitor cannot replicate this timing without the data. |
| 6. Remote monitoring | Basic: anomaly alerts, status dashboards. | Advanced: feeds Streams 1-5 with operational intelligence. The monitoring platform becomes the nervous system of the entire service business. | Monitoring evolves from a standalone stream to the infrastructure that powers every other stream. |

### Industry benchmarks: digital service leaders

The building equipment industry (elevators, escalators, HVAC, physical security) provides the clearest benchmarks for digital service adoption because these products share characteristics with most B2B partner-delivered equipment: physically installed, requiring ongoing maintenance, with long lifecycles and high uptime expectations.

**KONE 24/7 Connected Services (elevator/escalator)**

KONE partnered with AWS to build an IoT platform targeting 1.6 million units in its maintenance base. The system monitors vibration, temperature, and door cycles (the leading cause of elevator failure) to predict issues before they cause disruption. Results: 70% increase in proactive fault identification, 40% fewer customer-reported callouts, 40% fewer entrapments. The mobile app provides building managers real-time status of every unit. KONE's next phase: digital twins using AWS IoT TwinMaker, modeling people flow at transit stations to optimize vertical transportation. KONE reports approximately 90% annual customer retention in service, driven by the switching cost of the connected platform.

**Otis ONE (elevator)**

Otis operates the largest installed base in the industry: 2.4 million units under contract. Service generates 60% of revenue but approximately 90% of operating profit, with service margins at 25.5% and expanding. Otis ONE uses IoT sensors and AI for predictive maintenance, sending a constant digital heartbeat from every connected elevator. The Gen360 platform replaces mechanical safety components with electronic ones, enabling remote resets and diagnostics. Otis is upselling digital monitoring subscriptions to building owners as a SaaS-like addon to traditional maintenance contracts, at margins higher than physical service delivery.

**Schindler Ahead (elevator)**

Schindler's digital platform focuses on the digital twin concept: the elevator talks to the cloud, which talks to the technician, who talks back to the elevator. Closed-loop diagnostics. Their measurement target: eliminating "running on arrival" calls where a technician shows up and the equipment is working (a ghost error). The ActionBoard dashboard prioritizes issues by business impact: is the freight elevator down during delivery hours?

**TK Elevator MAX (elevator)**

Built on Microsoft Azure. Measures the exact number of door open/close cycles to calculate when rollers will fail, replacing time-based schedules (e.g., replace every 3 years) with usage-based prediction. Marketed with a specific claim: MAX cuts downtime by up to 50%. Early mover in predictive maintenance, though KONE and Otis have since caught up on platform sophistication.

**Key metrics from the elevator industry (applicable to any B2B installed equipment):**

| Metric | What it measures | Good score |
|---|---|---|
| MTBC (Mean Time Between Callbacks) | Average days between unplanned service calls | >365 days (once a year or less) |
| Availability % | Time equipment is available for use | 99.5% (standard), 99.8% (premium) |
| MTTR (Mean Time To Repair) | Hours from failure report to operational restoration | <4 hours (urban) |
| Proactive fault identification rate | % of issues detected by monitoring before customer reports | >50% (Professional), >70% (World-class, KONE benchmark) |
| Connected fleet penetration | % of installed base with active IoT connectivity | >30% (Professional), >70% (World-class) |

**Negotiation insight for performance-based contracts:** ignore "99% uptime" guarantees in service contracts. They are too easy to manipulate. Instead, negotiate on MTBC targets: the monthly fee automatically decreases if breakdowns per unit per year exceed a defined threshold (e.g., more than 2). This aligns the manufacturer/partner's financial incentive with the customer's operational need.

### Install base as foundation

Every service motion starts with the install base register from Stage 5. Required fields: serial number, end-customer, partner, installation date, warranty expiration, service contract status and value, contract expiration, NPS score, expected lifecycle end date, service delivery model assignment. Incomplete data: fix first, sell second.

### Service contract lifecycle

| Phase | Timing | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attach at sale | Point of delivery | Offer maintenance contract during handover. Top performers: 31% higher attach rate (BCG). | Partner (sales) + manufacturer (templates, pricing) |
| Renewal pipeline | 365/180/90 days before expiration | Proactive outreach with updated terms and upsell (retrofit, upgrade, expanded coverage). | Partner (relationship) + partner manager (oversight) |
| Mid-contract service | Ongoing | Scheduled maintenance visits. Each visit: touchpoint for retrofit, parts, modernization signals. | Partner (Model 1) or manufacturer (Model 2) |
| Renewal | Before expiration | Negotiation: same terms, expanded scope, tier upgrade, multi-year. | Partner + manufacturer |
| Churn | At/after expiration | Root cause: competitive displacement, budget cut, end-of-life, service dissatisfaction, partner neglect. Distinguish partial churn (drops contract, keeps ad-hoc) from total churn (stops all service). | Partner manager (post-mortem) |

### Retrofit and modernization pipeline

Separate from renewal. These are capital opportunities from the installed base.

Retrofit triggers: new product generation, regulatory change, customer expansion, service-visit-identified degradation.

Modernization triggers: equipment at expected lifecycle end, TCO exceeds replacement cost, facility renovation, competitive displacement threat.

Pipeline management: retrofit tracked as partner-sourced in Stage 4. Modernization tracked as separate sub-pipeline. Both feed Stage 7 when they expand beyond original scope.

Proactive modernization: flag every unit by lifecycle end date. Trigger conversation 24 months before. The incumbent advantage is enormous when exercised early. The competitor's advantage is equally large when it is not.

### QBR framework for service

Covers all seven streams per partner.

Topics: install base summary and penetration, revenue by stream, renewal rate trend, churn root cause distribution, spare parts capture rate (BCG top performers: 16pp above average), retrofit and modernization pipeline, partner service performance (first-time fix rate, NPS, SLA compliance, certification status).

### Predictive churn modeling

Signals: NPS below average, support ticket spike, declining usage (IoT products), late payments, contact unresponsiveness, competitor activity, partner disengagement. Weighted composite score. At-risk accounts flagged for intervention. Requires 12+ months of Stages 5/6 data.

## Common failure modes

**Service as cost center.** Company views service as post-sale support, not revenue. Technicians fix problems but never identify retrofit opportunities. Every service visit is a missed sales call. Fix: train technicians to report expansion signals. One question per visit: "is there anything about this installation that no longer meets your needs?"

**Spare parts leakage.** Parts revenue lost to third-party distributors. Top performers capture 16pp more (BCG). Fix: competitive pricing, faster availability, warranty tied to genuine parts.

**No attach at sale.** Equipment delivered, no service contract attached. Customer forgets, uses a local provider, or calls the competitor. Top performers attach 31% above average (BCG). Fix: bundle service into equipment quote.

**Modernization left to chance.** Equipment hits end of life, competitor's partner is already on site. Fix: lifecycle end-date flag, modernization conversation 24 months before.

**Single-stream thinking.** Company manages maintenance contracts but ignores the other five streams. Service revenue plateaus because only one engine is running.

## Intervention library

### Play: service stream mapping and pricing
Decompose service revenue into seven streams. Current revenue, margin, partner role, pricing per stream. Identify dormant streams. Price them. Present to partners as business case. Source: BCG aftermarket benchmark. Lead time: 4 weeks analysis, 8 weeks partner enablement.

### Play: attach-at-sale program
Bundle maintenance contract into equipment quote. Train sales and partner teams on total-cost-of-ownership selling. First-year discount for attach. Track attach rate monthly. Source: BCG (31% outperformance on attach). Lead time: 2 weeks pricing, 4 weeks training.

### Play: spare parts capture program
Three levers: price competitiveness (benchmark vs third-party), availability (consignment inventory at certified partners, same/next-day for critical parts), warranty tie (validity requires genuine parts). Track capture rate quarterly. Source: BCG (16pp capture rate advantage, 50%+ margins). Lead time: 4 weeks pricing, 8 weeks consignment setup.

### Play: retrofit and modernization pipeline activation
Audit installed base for upgrade-eligible units. Publish upgrade paths by model with ROI. Train partners to propose during service visits. Flag lifecycle end dates, trigger modernization at 24 months. Track as separate pipeline. Source: BCG (green services reaching one-third of customers by 2027). Lead time: 6-8 weeks audit, pipeline impact within 2 quarters.

### Play: install base cleanup
Prerequisite. Extract 3 years of delivery records. Cross-reference finance, partner, and service records. 30-day cleanup sprint. Target: 90% completeness. Lead time: 4-6 weeks.

### Play: predictive churn model
World-class play. Composite risk score from NPS, tickets, usage, payments, responsiveness, competitive signals, partner engagement. 12+ months data required. Lead time: 8-12 weeks.

### Play: partner renewal incentive alignment
Equalize margins or add retention bonus. Strongest lever: renewal rate as tier criterion. Lead time: 30 days analysis, 90 days implementation.

### Play: connected fleet rollout (digital foundation)
- **Applicability:** companies with IoT-capable equipment but low connected fleet penetration (<30%). The prerequisite for all digital service streams.
- **Description:** prioritize connectivity rollout by install base value: connect the highest-revenue, highest-maintenance-cost units first. Bundle IoT activation into the next service visit (technician installs sensor and provisions the connection). Set a quarterly connectivity target. Track connected fleet penetration as a KPI. Do not attempt predictive maintenance or digital twin until the connected fleet reaches 30% (insufficient data for model training below this threshold).
- **Source:** KONE: migrated from third-party IoT to AWS-based platform to scale from 100K to 500K+ connected units. 99.9% provisioning success rate. Scaling on old architecture would have cost 3x more.
- **Lead time:** 4-8 weeks for pilot (50-100 units). 6-12 months for fleet-wide rollout depending on install base size and service visit frequency.

### Play: predictive maintenance launch
- **Applicability:** companies with 30%+ connected fleet and 6-12 months of monitoring data. The play that converts monitoring data into reduced downtime and higher contract value.
- **Description:** train predictive models on historical monitoring data: correlate sensor patterns (vibration, temperature, component cycles, usage frequency) with actual failure events. Start with the highest-cost failure mode (e.g., door mechanism for elevators, motor failure for revolving doors). Deploy predictions to the service team: alert X days before predicted failure, schedule preventive intervention. Measure: reduction in unplanned callouts, increase in proactive service visits, change in MTBC. Use the data to justify performance-based contract migration (Stream 1 World-class).
- **Source:** KONE: 70% increase in proactive fault identification. TK Elevator MAX: 50% downtime reduction. BCG: predictive maintenance reduces service events while enabling premium pricing.
- **Lead time:** 8-12 weeks for model training and pilot. 6 months for validated results on the first failure mode.

### Play: digital subscription product launch
- **Applicability:** companies with operational monitoring and predictive platforms looking to create a new revenue stream from digital services.
- **Description:** package the digital capabilities into tiered subscription products (Foundation: monitoring and alerts, Advanced: predictive maintenance, Premium: digital twin and advisory). Price per unit per month. Offer Foundation tier free for the first year to drive adoption (freemium), then charge for renewal and upsell to Advanced/Premium. Train partners to sell digital subscriptions alongside maintenance contracts. Track digital services revenue as a separate line. BCG: digital services must reach 10%+ of revenue to impact valuation.
- **Source:** Otis: upselling SaaS-like monitoring packages at margins higher than physical service. KPMG: 73% of manufacturers investing in XaaS. BCG: industrial SaaS yields higher margins and less-cyclical revenue.
- **Lead time:** 12-16 weeks for product definition, pricing, and partner enablement. First revenue within 6 months. Break-even on platform investment in 3-4 years (KPMG benchmark).

**RACI variant: companies without dedicated service operations**

In companies with fewer than 15 active partners and no separate service organization, the partner manager absorbs service pipeline oversight alongside relationship management. Service delivery depends entirely on the chosen model: in Model 1 (partner delivers), the partner handles all service execution and the manufacturer's role is parts supply and technical escalation. The partner manager tracks the renewal pipeline and service KPIs using the same QBR cadence as the sales relationship. In Model 2 (manufacturer delivers), the manufacturer's general operations or field service team handles execution. The scaling trigger for a dedicated service operations function: when service revenue exceeds 15% of total revenue or the install base exceeds 200 active units, a dedicated person to manage the seven-stream service business produces measurable ROI.
