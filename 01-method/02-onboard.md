# Stage 2: Onboard

## Strategic intent

Move a signed partner from contract to first revenue. Fast.

Onboarding is not orientation. It is the structured transfer of product knowledge, commercial terms, operational process, and system access that a partner needs to close their first deal without calling the manufacturer for help. The metric is time-to-first-revenue, not time-to-portal-login. Every day between contract signature and first closed deal is a day the recruitment investment earns zero return.

The failure rate in partner programs concentrates here. Industry data shows that 70% of channel partnerships fail. Most do not fail because the partner was wrong (that is a Stage 1 problem). They fail because the partner signed, received a welcome email, got access to a portal they never logged into, and quietly disengaged within 90 days. The manufacturer never noticed because no one was tracking activation. [P-48]

Onboarding is where asymmetric giving earns its return. The manufacturer invests first: training time, technical support, joint call support, demo equipment, first-deal hand-holding. This investment is not optional. It is the cost of converting a recruitment win into a revenue-producing relationship. Companies that treat onboarding as a cost center produce a partner base that treats the partnership as an afterthought. [P-47] [P-48]

## Maturity levels

| Level | Capabilities | KPIs |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | Product training within 30 days. Portal/system access within 5 business days. Named manufacturer contact for questions. | Time-to-first-revenue <365 days. Onboarding completion >50%. 90-day retention >70%. |
| Professional | Onboarding playbook with milestones and SLAs per partner type. Completion tracked; stalled partners flagged and contacted. First-deal support process documented. | Time-to-first-revenue <180 days. Onboarding completion >80%. 90-day retention >85%. |
| World-class | Onboarding automated and personalized per partner type. Partner satisfaction measured at 30 and 90 days. Time-to-first-revenue tracked with quarterly improvement targets. | Time-to-first-revenue <90 days. Onboarding completion >95%. 90-day retention >95%. |

## The system

### The 30-60-90 framework

Onboarding runs on a 30-60-90 day joint action plan. The plan is not a suggestion. It is a shared commitment between manufacturer and partner with specific milestones, deadlines, and ownership at each phase. Both sides sign it within 5 business days of contract execution.

**Days 1-30: foundation**

The partner achieves minimum viable knowledge. They can describe the product, identify a qualified opportunity, and navigate the manufacturer's systems.

| Milestone | Owner | Deadline | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portal access provisioned | Manufacturer ops | Day 1-3 | Login confirmed |
| Welcome call with assigned partner manager | Manufacturer | Day 3-5 | Call completed, joint plan signed |
| Product training: core modules completed | Partner | Day 14 | LMS completion record |
| Commercial terms reviewed and confirmed | Both | Day 14 | Signed acknowledgment |
| Deal registration process walked through | Manufacturer | Day 21 | Test registration submitted |
| First pipeline review: partner identifies 3-5 target accounts | Both | Day 30 | Accounts logged in CRM/PRM |

**Days 31-60: activation**

The partner pursues their first deal with manufacturer support. The manufacturer is actively involved: joint calls, proposal review, pricing support.

| Milestone | Owner | Deadline | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| First qualified opportunity registered | Partner | Day 45 | Deal registration in PRM |
| Joint customer call conducted | Both | Day 50 | Call logged, next steps documented |
| Proposal or quote submitted to end-customer | Partner (with manufacturer review) | Day 55 | Quote in system |
| Advanced training modules completed (if applicable) | Partner | Day 60 | LMS completion record |

**Days 61-90: first revenue**

The partner closes or advances their first deal. The manufacturer transitions from active support to on-demand support.

| Milestone | Owner | Deadline | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| First deal closed or in final negotiation | Partner | Day 75 | Opportunity stage in CRM |
| Onboarding satisfaction survey completed | Partner | Day 90 | Survey submitted |
| 90-day review call: performance, feedback, next quarter plan | Both | Day 90 | Review documented |
| Partner classified: activated (on track) or stalled (intervention needed) | Manufacturer | Day 90 | Status updated in PRM |

**The 90-day gate:** at Day 90, every partner falls into one of three categories. Activated: first deal closed or in advanced pipeline. Progressing: active engagement, pipeline building, but no close yet. Stalled: no meaningful activity after 90 days. Stalled partners receive a re-engagement intervention (see intervention library). Partners who remain stalled at Day 180 enter the exit evaluation process defined in Stage 1.

### Modular onboarding by partner type

Different partner types need different onboarding tracks. A reseller needs product knowledge and commercial terms. A system integrator needs technical integration depth and project management alignment. An LSP needs service protocols and parts access. A one-size-fits-all onboarding program wastes the SI's time on content they already know and underwhelms the reseller on content they need. [P-13]

| Partner type | Core modules (required) | Extended modules (type-specific) | Target time-to-first-revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reseller | Product overview, pricing, deal registration, portal navigation | Competitive positioning, objection handling, demo access | 60-90 days |
| System Integrator | Product overview, technical specifications, API/integration documentation | Solution architecture, project scoping, joint delivery model | 90-120 days |
| Local Service Provider | Product overview, installation procedures, service protocols | Parts ordering, warranty process, escalation paths, certification requirements | 90-120 days |
| Distributor | Product portfolio overview, pricing structure, logistics and ordering | Inventory management, credit terms, sub-partner onboarding support | 45-60 days |
| Technology partner | Product overview, integration specifications, API access | Joint solution development, co-marketing alignment, technical certification | 120-180 days |
| Referral partner | Product overview (condensed), ICP definition, referral submission process | N/A (lightest track) | 30-45 days |

Each track shares the same 30-60-90 structure but differs in content depth and milestone definitions. The LMS or onboarding platform delivers the right track based on partner type classification from Stage 1.

### First-deal support

The first deal is the critical conversion event. A partner who closes their first deal within 90 days has a fundamentally different trajectory than one who does not. The manufacturer's role during the first deal is active, not passive:

- Joint discovery call with the end-customer (manufacturer provides product expertise, partner provides customer relationship)
- Proposal review before submission (manufacturer checks configuration, pricing accuracy, competitive positioning)
- Pricing approval within 24 hours (slow pricing kills first deals faster than any competitor)
- Technical pre-sales support on demand (not scheduled for next week)
- Win/loss debrief regardless of outcome (the debrief is the learning, the deal is the context)

After the first deal closes, the manufacturer steps back progressively. The second deal gets lighter support. By the third deal, the partner operates independently or escalates only on exceptions. This graduated withdrawal is the design. Permanent hand-holding does not scale. Premature withdrawal loses the partner.

## Common failure modes

**Portal-as-onboarding.** The partner receives a login, a link to a content library, and a "let us know if you need anything" email. This is not onboarding. It is abandonment dressed as self-service. Self-service works at World-class maturity when the platform is sophisticated and the partner is experienced. It fails at Basic maturity because the partner does not know what they do not know.

**One-size-fits-all.** Every partner gets the same 90-minute webinar regardless of type, experience level, or commercial model. The SI already knows how to scope a project but needs technical integration documentation. The reseller needs competitive positioning but sits through an API walkthrough. Both disengage.

**No activation tracking.** The manufacturer tracks how many partners signed, not how many partners activated. A program with 200 signed partners and 30 active partners has a 15% activation rate, but the executive dashboard shows "200 partners" and everyone feels good. The 170 inactive partners are invisible cost: recruitment spend written off, onboarding resources consumed, support tickets filed by partners who will never produce revenue.

**Slow first-deal support.** Partner finds an opportunity, submits a deal registration, and waits 5 days for approval. Then requests pricing support and waits 3 days. Then requests a technical pre-sales call and gets scheduled for next week. By the time the manufacturer responds, the partner has either lost the deal or decided the partnership is not worth the friction.

**No 90-day gate.** Partners who stall are never flagged, never contacted, and never exited. They remain in the program as phantom partners, inflating the partner count while producing nothing. Without a defined gate, the stall is permanent.

## Diagnostic questions

1. What is your current time-to-first-revenue for new partners? Do you track it?
2. What percentage of partners who signed in the last 12 months have closed at least one deal?
3. Do you have different onboarding tracks for different partner types, or does everyone get the same program?
4. How do you support a partner's first deal? Is the support level documented or ad hoc?
5. What happens when a partner stalls during onboarding? Is there an intervention process?
6. How quickly do partners get portal/system access after signing?
7. Do you measure partner satisfaction with the onboarding experience?
8. What is your 90-day partner retention rate? 180-day?
9. Do you have a 30-60-90 joint action plan, or does onboarding end with a training session?
10. How do you decide when a partner has graduated from onboarding to self-sufficient operation?

## Intervention library

### Play: 30-60-90 joint action plan implementation

- **Applicability:** any company without a structured onboarding timeline. The foundational play for moving from Basic to Professional.
- **Description:** build the 30-60-90 template from the framework above. Customize milestone content per partner type. Assign ownership for each milestone. Implement tracking in PRM or a shared project management tool. The joint plan is co-signed by the partner within 5 days of contract. Both sides commit.
- **Source:** [P-48] (asymmetric giving). Partner Playbooks (30-60-90 as standard onboarding structure). PartnerStandard (full partner lifecycle model).
- **Lead time:** 2 weeks to build templates per type. 1 week to configure tracking. First partners onboarded under new framework within 30 days.

### Play: modular onboarding tracks

- **Applicability:** companies with more than one partner type whose current onboarding is a single generic program. Professional maturity play.
- **Description:** decompose the existing onboarding content into modules. Tag each module by partner type applicability. Build type-specific tracks in the LMS or onboarding platform. Each track inherits the 30-60-90 structure but differs in content depth. A reseller's Day 14 product training is not the same as an SI's Day 14 product training. Configure the LMS to deliver the right track based on partner type classification. Test with 5 partners per type before full rollout.
- **Source:** [P-13] (partner type segmentation). ZINFI (modular onboarding reduces activation timeline from 60-90 days to 15-30 days).
- **Lead time:** 4-6 weeks for content decomposition and track build. Impact measurable within one onboarding cohort.

### Play: activation tracking and stall intervention

- **Applicability:** any company that tracks partners signed but not partners activated. The play that makes invisible churn visible.
- **Description:** define "activated" with a binary criterion: has the partner registered at least one qualified deal within 90 days? Build a dashboard that shows activation rate by cohort (month of signing). Flag partners who miss the Day 30 and Day 60 milestones. Trigger an automatic re-engagement sequence: partner manager call at Day 35 (missed Day 30), escalation to channel leadership at Day 65 (missed Day 60), 90-day gate classification at Day 90. Stalled partners at Day 180 enter Stage 1 exit evaluation.
- **Source:** [P-40] (institutional capability). PartnerStack (network-approved partners 14x more likely to generate revenue: activation is the differentiator).
- **Lead time:** 2 weeks to define criteria and build dashboard. Immediate impact on visibility. Activation rate improvement within 2 cohorts.

### Play: first-deal acceleration

- **Applicability:** companies where time-to-first-revenue exceeds 180 days. The play that directly compresses the onboarding-to-revenue gap.
- **Description:** assign a dedicated first-deal support resource (can be a senior partner manager, a pre-sales engineer, or a rotating slot) to every new partner's first opportunity. The resource provides: joint discovery call within 48 hours of deal registration, proposal review within 24 hours, pricing approval within 4 hours during business hours, and win/loss debrief within 1 week of outcome. Track the number of first deals supported and the conversion rate versus partners without dedicated support. This play is resource-intensive for the first 90 days per partner and self-liquidating thereafter: a partner who closes their first deal fast requires less support on subsequent deals.
- **Source:** [P-48] (asymmetric giving). Allego (channel partner enablement: "coordinating between yourself, a partner, and a prospect is really hard; if you can make the process more agile, great partners are going to want to work with you").
- **Lead time:** Immediate if resources exist. Impact measurable on the first cohort.

### Play: onboarding experience optimization

- **Applicability:** companies at Professional maturity with established onboarding process looking to reach World-class. The infrastructure exists but satisfaction scores are flat or activation rates plateau.
- **Description:** implement partner satisfaction surveys at Day 30 and Day 90. Measure NPS or CSAT specifically for the onboarding experience. Correlate satisfaction scores with activation speed and first-year revenue. Identify the modules, processes, or interactions that correlate with high satisfaction and fast activation. Double down on those. Identify the friction points that correlate with low satisfaction and stalling. Eliminate or redesign them. Conduct quarterly onboarding retrospectives with the last cohort's feedback. Treat the onboarding experience as a product with a continuous improvement cycle.
- **Source:** [P-41] (performance management is bidirectional). Research shows onboarding friction is the primary driver of early partner churn, not product-market fit.
- **Lead time:** 2 weeks to build surveys and implement. First data point at Day 30 of next cohort. Meaningful trend data after 3 cohorts.

## RACI within the stage

| Activity | Partner manager | Partner ops / enablement | Sales leadership | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joint action plan creation and signing | R, A | C (template) | I | R (co-signer) |
| Portal/system access provisioning | I | R, A | I | I (receives access) |
| Product training delivery | C | R, A | I | R (completes training) |
| Commercial terms review | R, A | C | C (pricing authority) | R (acknowledges) |
| First-deal support (joint calls, proposal review) | R | C (technical pre-sales) | C (pricing approval) | R, A (owns customer relationship) |
| Activation tracking and stall flagging | I | R, A (dashboard) | I | N/A |
| Stall intervention (re-engagement calls) | R, A | I | C (escalation) | Target |
| 90-day review and classification | R, A | C (data) | I | R (participates in review) |
| Onboarding satisfaction survey | I | R, A (survey delivery) | I | R (completes survey) |

**RACI variant: companies without dedicated partner operations or enablement**

In companies where the partner team is one or two people, the partner manager absorbs operations and enablement responsibilities. The RACI compresses:

| Activity | First partner hire / CRO | Sales team | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joint action plan | R, A | C | R (co-signer) |
| System access | R, A (or delegate to IT) | I | I |
| Training delivery | R, A (can use recorded content to scale) | C (product knowledge input) | R |
| First-deal support | R (joint calls), CRO (pricing approval) | R (may co-sell) | R, A |
| Activation tracking | R, A (spreadsheet is acceptable at <25 partners) | I | N/A |
| 90-day review | R, A | I | R |

The scaling trigger remains 25-30 active partners. Below that threshold, a single person with discipline and the 30-60-90 framework produces better results than a two-person team without structure. Above that threshold, the partner manager cannot simultaneously onboard new partners and manage existing relationships. That is when partner operations becomes a separate function.
