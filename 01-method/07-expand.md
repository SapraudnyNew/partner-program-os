# Stage 7: Expand

## Strategic intent

Cross-sell, upsell, and open new segments or geographies through the partner portfolio. This is compound interest on the partnership investment.

Expand is the stage that separates mature partner programs from programs that plateau. A company that recruits, onboards, enables, co-sells, delivers, and renews has a functioning partner program. A company that also expands through its partner base has an ecosystem-led growth engine. The difference: a functioning program adds revenue linearly (more partners, more deals). An expansion engine multiplies revenue through the existing base (more products per partner, more segments per partner, more geographies per partner).

Expansion requires portfolio-level thinking. The question is not "how do I get Partner A to sell more?" The question is "across my entire partner portfolio, where are the coverage gaps, which partners can fill them, and which new markets can I enter through existing relationships without recruiting new partners?" This is constellation management: managing the portfolio as a system, not a collection of individual relationships. [P-14]

Expansion also depends on upstream data. The install base captured in Stage 5 and maintained through Stage 6's seven service streams identifies which end-customers use which products, which service contracts are active, and which equipment is approaching end of lifecycle. Cross-sell recommendations come from install base analysis: Customer X has Product A but not Product B. The partner who delivered Product A is positioned to propose Product B. Stage 6 retrofit and modernization pipelines bridge directly into expansion: a retrofit project that installs new modules is an expansion event. A modernization that replaces end-of-lifecycle equipment with current-generation product is both a Stage 6 renewal and a Stage 7 expansion. Without install base data and service stream visibility, expansion is guesswork. [P-44]

## The system

### Portfolio coverage mapping

Before expanding, map what the current partner portfolio covers and where the gaps are.

**Coverage matrix:**

| Dimension | Current coverage | Gap | Expansion opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Products sold through channel | [List products currently sold through partners] | [Products not yet channelized] | Which existing partners have the capability to sell additional products? |
| Customer segments | [Segments currently served by partners] | [Segments with no partner coverage] | Which existing partners serve adjacent segments? |
| Geographies | [Countries/regions where partners are active] | [Target geographies without partner presence] | Which existing partners have offices or customers in target geographies? |
| Verticals | [Industries where partners operate] | [Target verticals without partner coverage] | Which existing partners specialize in adjacent verticals? |
| Service capabilities | [Services partners currently deliver] | [Services needed but not covered by partner base] | Which partners could develop new service capabilities with enablement investment? |

The coverage matrix is maintained quarterly and reviewed at the portfolio level, not the individual partner level. It is the input to expansion decisions: recruit new partners for coverage gaps, or develop existing partners to fill them.

### Cross-sell and upsell through install base

Install base analysis identifies expansion opportunities at the account level:

**Cross-sell:** end-customer has Product A. Product B is complementary. The partner who delivered Product A proposes Product B. The trust is established, the relationship exists, and the partner understands the customer's environment.

**Upsell:** end-customer has Product A in a basic configuration. A premium configuration, a service upgrade, or additional units would serve the customer better. The partner proposes the upgrade during a service visit or QBR.

**Expansion pipeline:**

| Source | Identification method | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Install base cross-sell | Automated recommendation: customers with Product A but not Product B | Partner ops generates list. Partner manager assigns to partner. Partner executes. |
| Service-led upsell | Service technician or partner identifies expansion need during site visit | Partner (captures opportunity). Partner manager tracks in pipeline. |
| QBR-identified | Renewal QBR surfaces growth opportunities in existing accounts | Partner manager and partner jointly plan. |
| Account mapping-driven | Account mapping (Stage 4) reveals partner's customer with expansion potential | Partner manager coordinates. |
| Retrofit-originated | Stage 6 retrofit project reveals need for additional products or expanded scope | Partner (identifies during retrofit). Tracked as expansion when scope exceeds original installation. |
| Modernization-originated | Stage 6 modernization replaces end-of-lifecycle equipment. Often triggers adjacent product purchases. | Partner (manages relationship). Bridges Stage 6 pipeline to Stage 7. |
| Digital services upsell | Stage 6 IoT/monitoring data reveals underperforming or underutilized installations where additional products improve outcomes | Partner ops (generates insight). Partner (proposes to customer). |

Expansion pipeline is tracked separately from new-logo pipeline and from renewal pipeline. Three distinct pipelines, three distinct forecasts, three distinct cadences. Mixing them obscures which growth lever is working.

### New market entry through partners

The cheapest way to enter a new geography or vertical is through a partner who already operates there. The partner has the customer relationships, the regulatory knowledge, the local language capability, and the service infrastructure. The manufacturer provides the product, the brand, and the commercial terms.

**New market entry decision framework:**

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Do we have an existing partner in the target market? | Develop that partner for the new product/segment (Enable investment). | Recruit a new partner (return to Stage 1). |
| Does the existing partner have the right capability? | Proceed with expansion plan. | Invest in enablement (Stage 3) to build capability. |
| Is the market opportunity large enough to justify dedicated investment? | Create a dedicated expansion plan with the partner, including JBP targets and MDF. | Add to the partner's existing plan as an optional growth area. |
| Can the existing partner's coverage overlap with our target accounts in the new market? | Run account mapping (Stage 4) to identify specific opportunities. | Assess whether the partner's coverage is addressable with ABSM campaigns. |

### Constellation management

At World-class maturity, expansion is managed at the portfolio level, not the partner level. Constellation management means:

**Partner-to-partner referrals.** Partner A serves Vertical 1 but not Vertical 2. Partner B serves Vertical 2. When Partner A's customer needs a solution in Vertical 2, Partner A refers to Partner B. The manufacturer facilitates the referral, tracks it, and credits both partners. This turns the partner portfolio into a self-reinforcing network. [P-14]

**Coverage gap closing.** The portfolio coverage matrix identifies gaps. The manufacturer decides whether to fill each gap by developing an existing partner (cheaper, slower) or recruiting a new partner (faster, more expensive). The decision factors: size of the opportunity in the gap, capability distance for existing partners, availability of recruitment candidates.

**Portfolio rebalancing.** Partners who underperform in their assigned segments may be better suited to adjacent segments they have grown into. Annual portfolio review reassesses partner-to-segment assignments based on actual performance data, not original recruitment assumptions.

## Common failure modes

**Single-product partners.** Partners sell one product and never expand. The manufacturer never proposes additional products, and the partner never asks. The attach rate stays at 1.0 permanently. The fix: product expansion targets in the JBP, enablement on the second product, and margin incentive for multi-product deals.

**Geography-blind portfolio.** The manufacturer knows which countries partners operate in but does not map coverage gaps systematically. Expansion into new geographies defaults to recruiting new partners when an existing partner has an office in the target country. The fix: the coverage matrix.

**Expansion = more of the same.** The company defines expansion as "sell more of the same product to more of the same type of customer." This is scaling, not expanding. Expansion means new products, new segments, new geographies, or new service layers through the existing partner base. The distinction matters because scaling hits a ceiling (the addressable market for one product in one segment) while expansion opens new addressable markets.

**No install base visibility.** The company cannot identify which customers have which products. Cross-sell recommendations are impossible without this data. The fix: Stage 5 install base capture discipline, enforced at every handover.

**Partner hoarding.** Individual partner managers protect "their" partners from being developed by other product teams or in other geographies. The partner serves one product in one territory permanently. The fix: portfolio-level management with a partner development plan that crosses product and geography boundaries.

## Diagnostic questions

1. What percentage of your partners sell more than one of your products?
2. Do you have a coverage map that shows which partners serve which segments and geographies?
3. How do you identify cross-sell opportunities in the install base? Is it systematic or ad hoc?
4. Have you entered a new market through an existing partner in the past 12 months?
5. Do you track expansion revenue separately from new-logo and renewal revenue?
6. Do your partners refer business to each other? Do you facilitate and track this?
7. When was the last time you reviewed your partner portfolio at the portfolio level (not individual partner reviews)?
8. Do partner account plans include expansion targets (new products, new segments)?
9. How do you decide whether to develop an existing partner for a new market or recruit a new one?
10. What is your partner attach rate (average products per partner)?

## Intervention library

### Play: portfolio coverage mapping

- **Applicability:** any company that has not mapped its partner portfolio against products, segments, geographies, and verticals. The foundation for all expansion decisions.
- **Description:** build the coverage matrix from this document. Populate with current partner data. Identify the top 3 coverage gaps by revenue opportunity. For each gap, determine whether to develop an existing partner or recruit. Present to channel leadership with investment recommendations.
- **Source:** [P-14] (constellation thinking: portfolio, not individual). Atluri/Dietz (ecosystem strategy: coverage and orchestration).
- **Lead time:** 2-3 weeks for initial mapping. Quarterly refresh thereafter.

### Play: install base cross-sell activation

- **Applicability:** companies with install base data (from Stage 5) but no systematic cross-sell motion.
- **Description:** analyze the install base for cross-sell gaps: customers with Product A but not Product B. Generate a list per partner: "these are your customers who could benefit from additional products." Include the list in the QBR. Set a cross-sell pipeline target per partner. Track as expansion pipeline. [P-44]
- **Source:** Daikin model: install base management driving service and product expansion revenue.
- **Lead time:** 2 weeks for analysis. Impact on pipeline within one quarter.

### Play: partner-to-partner referral program

- **Applicability:** companies at Professional maturity with 20+ active partners across multiple segments or geographies. The play that turns the portfolio into a network.
- **Description:** design a referral mechanism: when Partner A identifies an opportunity outside their capability or coverage, they refer it to Partner B. The manufacturer orchestrates the handoff. Both partners receive credit: Partner A gets a referral fee, Partner B gets the deal. Track referral volume, conversion rate, and revenue generated from partner-to-partner referrals. Start with the 5 partners most likely to generate and receive referrals based on coverage complementarity.
- **Source:** [P-14] (constellation management). Bamford (alliance portfolio management: partners as a network).
- **Lead time:** 4 weeks for program design. 90 days for first referrals. Meaningful volume within 2-3 quarters.

### Play: existing partner new-market development

- **Applicability:** companies with a coverage gap in a geography or segment where an existing partner already operates.
- **Description:** select the partner best positioned to fill the gap (already has office, customers, or capability in the target market). Build a dedicated expansion plan: enablement investment (Stage 3) for the new product or segment, dedicated support for the first 3 deals, MDF for market entry campaigns, JBP targets for the new market. Track as a separate expansion initiative with its own pipeline and review cadence. Faster and cheaper than recruiting a new partner if the capability gap is bridgeable.
- **Source:** [P-10] (Build/Acquire/Partner: developing an existing partner is the partner equivalent of building capability).
- **Lead time:** 4-8 weeks for enablement and planning. First deals in the new market within 6 months.

### Play: portfolio rebalancing review

- **Applicability:** companies at Professional maturity with 12+ months of performance data per partner. The play that reallocates resources from underperforming assignments to higher-potential ones.
- **Description:** annual review of partner-to-segment assignments. For each partner: compare assigned segment with actual revenue distribution. Partners who generate 80% of their revenue from a different segment than originally assigned should be formally reassigned. Partners who underperform in their assigned segment but overperform in an adjacent segment should be redirected. Partners who underperform across all segments enter the Stage 1 exit evaluation. The review prevents the portfolio from calcifying around outdated assumptions.
- **Source:** [P-14] (constellation thinking). [P-15] (exit planning: graceful exit for partners who no longer fit).
- **Lead time:** 2-3 weeks for analysis. Annual cadence.

| Activity | Channel leadership | Partner manager | Product / segment leadership | Partner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portfolio coverage mapping | R, A | C (partner data) | C (product/segment priorities) | I |
| Coverage gap prioritization | R, A | I | R (revenue opportunity sizing) | I |
| Install base cross-sell list generation | C | R, A | C (product recommendations) | I (receives list) |
| Cross-sell execution | I | C (pipeline tracking) | I | R, A (owns customer relationship) |
| New market development planning | R, A | R (partner coordination) | C (product readiness) | R (co-plans) |
| Partner-to-partner referral facilitation | C | R, A (orchestrates handoff) | I | R (refers and receives) |
| Portfolio rebalancing review | R, A | C (performance data) | C (segment priorities) | I (receives updated assignment) |

**RACI variant: companies without dedicated channel leadership**

In pre-scale companies, expansion is owned by the CRO or the first partner hire as a secondary responsibility after managing existing partner relationships. Portfolio coverage mapping runs on a spreadsheet. Cross-sell analysis is manual (partner manager reviews install base and identifies gaps). Partner-to-partner referrals happen informally rather than through a structured program. The scaling trigger for dedicated expansion management: when the partner portfolio exceeds 30 partners or when the company identifies 3+ coverage gaps that existing partners cannot fill, expansion planning justifies dedicated attention. Until then, the most effective expansion motion is the QBR-identified cross-sell: the partner manager raises expansion opportunities during the quarterly review with each top partner.
